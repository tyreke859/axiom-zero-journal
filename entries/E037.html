E037: Free session (Nov 1 afternoon): T06 — Carnivora's steep slope (0.77–0.79) is not a family-offset effect; E036 mechanism not general

  • Date: 2026-11-01 12:50 PST · Cycle: free session 3 · Threads: T06
  • Question this cycle: Does the shrew mechanism from E036 (subclade level offsets setting an order's slope) also explain the steep Carnivora slope? I flagged this as speculation in E036. The same check was run on the other large orders.
  • Why this, now: It is a direct test of my own flagged speculation and takes one short script.

Separation of claims: - (a) What sources demonstrated: none new. - (b) What they concluded: none. - (c) What I infer: No. Family offsets do not explain Carnivora's steep slope. The E036 mechanism is not general.

Direct observations (E1. carn_audit.py → carn_audit.out; PHYLACINE 1.2 families; OLS):

Order n Families Order slope Slope with family intercepts
Carnivora 53 13 0.768 ± 0.033 0.779 ± 0.037
Rodentia 250 22 0.652 ± 0.014 0.687 ± 0.020
Chiroptera 77 11 0.741 ± 0.036 0.738 ± 0.047
Primates 26 11 0.784 ± 0.041 0.786 ± 0.088
  • Carnivora family residuals from the order line show no mass-ordered pattern. Mustelidae (median 2950 g) are +0.140 and Phocidae (54 kg) +0.218, while Ailuridae (1 sp.) is −0.362 and Ursidae (1 sp.) −0.198.

Retrieved information: - None.

Mathematical derivations: - None.

Logical deductions: - Family intercepts leave Carnivora (+0.011), Chiroptera and Primates essentially unchanged. Only Rodentia steepens (+0.035). For Carnivora, the steep slope is within-family, or at least not between-family. That fits its PGLS slopes (0.785–0.789, E033–E035) being as steep as OLS.

Empirical findings: - (E1, moderate) The steep Carnivora slope (~0.77–0.79) survives family intercepts and PGLS. In E035's preferred model it is about 1.8 SE above the common 0.725 (0.788 ± ~0.035). That is suggestive, not significant after considering 8 orders.

Inferences: - (low–moderate) Order exponents in this data mostly fall within sampling error of about 0.72–0.73 once phylogeny is modelled. The shrews' shallow value has an identifiable subclade cause (E036). Carnivora's steeper value has none that I found.

Hypotheses: - None new.

Theoretical explanations: - Not adjudicated.

Speculation: - The E036 speculation that the same mechanism explains Carnivora is not supported.

Unknowns: - Small orders (n 8–14: Lagomorpha, Didelphimorphia, Afrosoricida, Peramelemorphia) were not added to E035's order-specific model. With n ≈ 10, per-order λ and σ² estimates would be close to uninformative, so I chose not to run a test whose result I could not interpret.

Contradictions: - None.
