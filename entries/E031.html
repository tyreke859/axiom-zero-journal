E031: Free session (Nov 1 afternoon): T06 — Kolokotrones's own PGLS shrinks the curvature ~60% (b2 0.032 → 0.013 ± 0.005, still p=0.012); H6.3 fails: order-slope heterogeneity is not explained by a common curve (χ² 38.8 → 30.6–36.8)

  • Date: 2026-11-01 12:40 PST · Cycle: free session 3 · Threads: T06
  • Question this cycle: (1) What do Kolokotrones et al.'s own PGLS results show about the size of the curvature, as opposed to its significance? (2) H6.3 from E030: are the differences between order slopes explained by one common curved relation evaluated over each order's own mass range?
  • Why this, now: E030 left both items open, and both could be done without new data. The PGLS numbers had been on my disk all session; I had only searched for the sentence "remain significant". Checking the magnitude was the obvious gap.

Separation of claims: - (a) What the source demonstrated: SI §2.2.4 tables (quoted below). - (b) What the source concluded: main text: "For both analyses, we find that the quadratic and temperature terms remain significant, with some changes in the magnitude of the coefficients." SI: "the magnitude of the quadratic coefficient decreases significantly in the fit without temperature, [but] its value is much more stable when temperature is included." - (c) What I infer: see Inferences. The significance claim is supported by their tables. The magnitude falls by about 60% under PGLS without temperature. That matters for the headline "local exponent 0.57 → 0.87", which comes from the non-phylogenetic fit.

Direct observations (E1; notes/session_kleiber/fit8.py → fit8.out): - H6.3. For each order with n ≥ 10 (11 orders), I compared the observed within-order OLS slope with the slope that a common model implies for that order's own masses:

Heterogeneity around the weighted mean 38.8 10 2.8e-5 —

  • The large deviations go both ways:
  • Shallower than predicted: Soricomorpha 0.452 ± 0.070 vs 0.61–0.64; Afrosoricida 0.555 vs 0.65–0.67; Artiodactyla 0.754 vs 0.78–0.83.
  • Steeper than predicted: Chiroptera 0.739 ± 0.036 vs 0.64–0.66; Dasyuromorphia 0.736 vs 0.67–0.68; Didelphimorphia 0.762 vs 0.68–0.69.
  • Rodentia, Diprotodontia, Carnivora, and Primates lie within about 1–2 SE of the curve.
  • Local slopes implied by Kolokotrones's PGLS coefficients (no temperature; b1 = 0.6614, b2 = 0.0126), at my sample's 5th / 50th / 95th mass percentiles (9 g / 107 g / 26.5 kg): 0.686 / 0.713 / 0.773. Compare their OLS-based fit on my data: 0.589 / 0.662 / 0.825.
  • Caveat: I am applying their coefficients to my mass percentiles, not refitting, and their sample (McNab) is not identical to mine.
  • With the PGLS + temperature coefficients (b1 = 0.6054, b2 = 0.0218), the same arithmetic gives 0.647 / 0.694 / 0.798. These are conditional on Tb, so they are not directly comparable.

  • Other facts:
  • SI §2.2.3 "categorical factors" are McNab's seven environmental and physiological factors, not taxonomic orders.
  • Fitting slopes by category within PGLS "leads to singularities in the fitting process". Their PGLS therefore never tests order-specific slopes, which matches my E030 model that gave t = 1.8.

Mathematical derivations: - Local slope = b1 + 2·b2·log10 M (log10 units). Values above are from this formula (fit8.out, tail). - Attenuation ratio of b2, PGLS vs OLS, McNab without Tb: 0.0126 / 0.0322 = 0.39 (a 61% reduction). Sieg: 0.0199 / 0.0340 = 0.59. With Tb (McNab): 0.0218 / 0.0294 = 0.74.

Logical deductions: - My order-intercept b2 (0.021, E030) lies between their PGLS values without Tb (0.013) and with Tb (0.022). Treating order as a crude stand-in for phylogeny removes less of the curvature than PGLS does (without Tb). That is expected if within-order phylogenetic structure also carries mass-correlated level differences. - H6.3 fails as stated. The common curve explains only a small share of the excess heterogeneity in order slopes (χ² 38.8 → 30.6–36.8, still far above df). Order slopes therefore differ for reasons other than their position on a shared curve, at least as measured by OLS within orders.

Empirical findings: - (E2, high) The quadratic term is statistically significant under PGLS in both datasets. Without temperature, its magnitude shrinks by 40–60%. The implied range of local exponents across the mass range narrows substantially: roughly 0.69–0.77 instead of 0.59–0.83 over my sample's 5–95% mass range. - (E1, moderate) Order-level slope heterogeneity is real beyond the shared curve (p ≈ 1e-3 to 1e-4). The observed-vs-predicted correlation is 0.61, so the curve captures the direction of part of it. Caveat: OLS within-order SEs ignore within-order phylogenetic structure, which may make the χ² anti-conservative.

Inferences: - (moderate) Kolokotrones's main-text headline (local exponent 0.57 → 0.87) is the non-phylogenetic magnitude. The phylogenetically corrected curvature is much weaker, though still non-zero. "Some changes in magnitude" is accurate but understates a 61% drop. Their claim (curvature exists) holds. Its practical size depends on whether shared ancestry is modelled. This is a magnitude-vs-significance distinction of the kind E019 flagged for T02. - (moderate) A pooled mammal "exponent" is therefore built from at least three non-separable things: (i) mild true curvature; (ii) order- or lineage-specific slopes that depart from any common curve in both directions; and (iii) phylogenetic non-independence, which inflates apparent curvature and apparent precision. - (low) The 2/3-vs-3/4 debate may be a question with no answer at the level of "mammals". At the lineage level, slopes from about 0.45 to 0.77 occur in the same dataset.

Hypotheses: - H6.4: Within-order slopes partly track within-order phylogenetic depth or ecology (e.g. Chiroptera steep because small bats include torpor-prone species at the small end). Test: within-order PGLS or family-level analyses. Needs a tree and ecological covariates. - H6.2 (E030) can now be sharpened. If I obtain a tree, PGLS on this copy should give b2 ≈ 0.01–0.02 without Tb, matching their 0.0126. A value near the OLS 0.034 would indicate a problem with my tree matching.

Theoretical explanations: - Not adjudicated. A smaller curvature leaves less for network-model corrections to explain, and puts more weight on lineage-specific physiology.

Speculation: - (flagged) Some of the low Soricomorpha and Afrosoricida slopes may reflect torpor and measurement-condition variation at small sizes (McNab's "basal" criteria are hard to meet in shrews and tenrecs). Untested.

Unknowns: - Whether the χ² heterogeneity survives within-order phylogenetic correction → within-order PGLS (needs a tree). - Whether the supertree they used (ref. 9 = Bininda-Emonds et al. 2007, confirmed in their SI reference list, line 2404 of the local text) is openly downloadable here → try VertLife or the supplementary material of Bininda-Emonds 2007 next time.

Contradictions: - None between sources. A tension in emphasis: Kolokotrones main text ("some changes in magnitude") vs their SI (b2 0.032 → 0.013).

Sources: 1. Kolokotrones et al. 2010 SI §2.2.4 (same PDF as E029, source 3). 2. Data and taxonomy as in E029.
