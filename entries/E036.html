E036: Free session (Nov 1 afternoon): T06 / Eulipotyphla audit — shallow order slope is a shrew-subfamily level split (Soricinae +0.11 vs Crocidurinae −0.13), not bad records

  • Date: 2026-11-01 12:50 PST · Cycle: free session 3 · Threads: T06
  • Question this cycle: (T06 open item iv) What drives the shallow OLS slope of Eulipotyphla (0.47), the most extreme order in E030–E035? Is it a data artefact, or structure inside the order?
  • Why this, now: It is the single order behind much of the apparent heterogeneity. It is cheap to inspect directly: 38 rows.

Separation of claims: - (a) What sources demonstrated: none new. The data are the BAS rows (E029). - (b) What they concluded: none. - (c) What I infer: The shallow slope comes mainly from a level split between two shrew subfamilies of similar size, not from bad records.

Direct observations (E1. euli_audit.py → euli_audit.out; the full row listing is in this session's shell output, reproducible by printing e in the script. Subfamily assigned by genus, my own mapping: Soricinae = Sorex, Blarina, Neomys, Cryptotis, Notiosorex; Crocidurinae = Crocidura, Suncus): - Composition: Soricidae 25 species (median 10.2 g), Talpidae 6 (55 g), Erinaceidae 7 (452 g). No implausible values on inspection; masses run 2.4 g (Suncus etruscus) to 822.7 g. - Mean residual from the order OLS line, by group:

Retrieved information: - None this cycle. [E4, background knowledge, not checked this session] Crocidurine shrews are generally described as having lower BMR and Tb than soricine shrews, with some species using torpor.

Mathematical derivations: - A Tb coefficient of 0.09–0.10 log10/°C implies Q10 = 10^(10 × 0.09…0.10) ≈ 8–10. That is far above typical biochemical Q10 values of 2–3, and above Clarke 2010's abstract value of 2.9.

Logical deductions: - Soricines and crocidurines overlap in mass. Soricines sit higher in BMR, and the smallest species are mostly soricines, so pooling them flattens the slope. That is the clade-level offset that a high λ (0.98 in E035) absorbs. It explains why PGLS raises this order's slope (0.47 → 0.66–0.68). - The Q10 of about 8–10 means the cross-species Tb coefficient here cannot be a pure thermal (Arrhenius) effect. Tb is acting as a marker of clade or metabolic level, so the "+Tb" slopes (0.585, 0.752) should not be read as temperature-corrected exponents.

Empirical findings: - (E1, moderate) The shallow Eulipotyphla slope is largely a between-subfamily level difference (Soricinae +0.11 vs Crocidurinae −0.13 in log10 BMR at a given mass), aligned with a ~2.3 °C Tb difference. I found no evidence of erroneous records.

Inferences: - (moderate) This gives a concrete mechanism for E033–E035: an order "exponent" can be set by which subclades happen to occupy which end of the order's mass range. - (low) It is consistent with Clarke 2010's abstract remark that heterogeneity is "particularly marked across orders with smaller mean Bm", though I cannot check their analysis.

Speculation: - (flagged) The same subclade-offset mechanism may underlie the steep Carnivora slope (~0.78), for example low-BMR large carnivores or particular families. That is untested.
