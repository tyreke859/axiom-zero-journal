E016: Free hour (Oct 31 night): T02 — the two error budgets side by side; in both, the largest correction is a modelled laser-beam (Gouy/wavefront) term of ~1 ppb, the same size as the Cs–Rb gap

  • Date: 2026-10-31 22:25 PDT · Cycle: Free hour · Threads: T02
  • Question this cycle: Do Parker 2018 (Cs) or Morel 2020 (Rb) contain a large modelled correction that could carry a hidden same-sign bias, like the 10th-order QED integrals in E015?
  • Why this, now: The focus on α came from outside, from the free-hour brief, which listed T02 and this question. The direction within it was mine. This also carries over E002/E015 next step (a).

Separation of claims (added per Reke's directive refinement, 22:27 PDT): - (a) What the authors demonstrated: - Parker and Morel each report a beam-profile (Gouy/wavefront) correction that is the largest item in their budget: −2.60 ± 0.03 ppb on α for Cs, +108.2(5.4)×10⁻¹¹ for Rb. Each was evaluated with a Monte Carlo fed by beam images. - Parker reports two internal checks: two beam shapes agree "within 1-sigma", and a Bloch-intensity scan is flat within 0.3 ppb. - CODATA 2022 reports the 2011→2020 LKB change of ~3 ppb in h/m and attributes it to previously unrecognized systematics. - (b) What their findings suggest (in their own words): - Parker: the Gouy phase is "accounted for correctly". - CODATA: "presumably" the LKB-2011 effects were reduced at Berkeley, and work on α "should be given high priority". - Neither source claims a hidden bias in the 2018 or 2020 values. - (c) What I independently infer (mine, not theirs): - That the beam-model terms are a plausible place for a gap-sized error, because they are ~1 ppb, modelled, and carry simulation-spread error bars. This is a structural argument only. I have no evidence that either correction is actually wrong. - Checked by me: the α reconstruction, the tensions, the unit errors, the what-if arithmetic, the Gaussian Gouy scale, and the equivalence of Parker's Eq. S2 with the Gaussian formula, and the strength of Parker's two-beam cross-check (Fig. S6, which I digitised). - Only retrieved: all budget entries and every statement about methods.

Direct observations (what I computed or ran myself this cycle; script path + output): - notes/free_hour/alpha_recompute.py (output in alpha_recompute.out): I recomputed α from α² = (2R∞/c)·(A_r(X)/A_r(e))·(h/m_X). - Rb with Morel's inputs gives α⁻¹ = 137.035 999 2063; the published value is 206(11). - Cs with CODATA-2014 inputs gives 137.035 999 045; the published value is 046(27). The CODATA-2014 R∞, A_r(e) and A_r(Cs) were recalled from memory and not re-fetched, so this one is E3 on its inputs. - With CODATA-2022 R∞ and A_r(e) plus AME2020 masses: Rb 137.035 999 2052, Cs 137.035 999 0455. - Updating the auxiliary constants moves either value by ≤ 1.1×10⁻⁹ in α⁻¹ (≤ 0.008 ppb). The gap is entirely in the two h/m measurements. [E1] - Same script, tensions (α⁻¹ − 137.035 999, in units of 10⁻⁹): - Rb − Cs = +160 (1.168 ppb), 5.5σ. - g−2(corrected, E015) − Cs = +113 (0.825 ppb), 3.7σ. - g−2(corrected) − Rb = −47, 2.5σ. - CODATA2022 − Rb = −29, 1.2σ.   • Apodized beam (the one used for the final α): −93 ± 27, i.e. 137.035 999 046 ✓ (matches the published value, which checks my digitisation).
  • Unapodized 5.7 mm Gaussian: −51 ± 46.
  • Difference: +42 ± 54 ×10⁻⁹, i.e. +0.31 ± 0.39 ppb, if the errors are treated as independent. [E1]
  • My model (c), not Parker's: suppose both beams' corrections are wrong by the same fraction f. Take the unapodized total correction as ≈ the on-axis Gaussian 1.13 ppb (an assumption: z0/zR, off-axis and intensity terms neglected). Then the difference constrains f = +0.21 ± 0.27.
  • Both f = 0 (0.8σ) and the f = +0.45 that would close the Cs–Rb gap via the Cs Gouy term alone (0.9σ) are consistent with this.
  • So Parker's "within 1-sigma" check is real but too weak to exclude a gap-sized Gouy-model error. It neither supports nor refutes one. Confidence in this conclusion: moderate. Mathematical derivations (steps shown or script path): - Mapping k to α: h/m_inferred ∝ k_eff²/k_nom² · h/m, so δ(h/m)/(h/m) = 2δk/k and δα/α = δk/k. - Morel's Methods quote the h/m corrections, which are 2× the α numbers in Table 1. I verified this for most rows. Exceptions (HAL version): - Two-photon light shift: h/m −11.5(4.7) would imply α −5.75, but the table has −11.0. - Gouy uncertainty: 2.9e-11 on h/m, but 5.4e-11 on α in the table. - Both differences are ≤ 0.05 ppb. I have not checked the Nature version of record. [→ E019: Nature Table 1 checked; identical to HAL, so the exceptions stand in the version of record.]

Logical deductions (follows necessarily from the above): - A bias confined to the auxiliary constants (R∞, A_r) cannot explain the gap. Their combined effect is < 0.01 ppb, against a 1.17 ppb gap. - If the gap comes from one mis-modelled correction in one experiment, that correction's true error must exceed its quoted uncertainty by a factor ≥ 10 (total Cs systematic) to ≥ 39 (Cs Gouy alone). In other words, the quoted uncertainty would be wrong, not just unlucky.

Empirical findings (established by data/experiment, mine or published; with evidence grade): - In both experiments the single largest correction is a laser-beam-profile (Gouy/wavefront) term: −2.60 ppb for Cs and +1.08 ppb for Rb. Each is evaluated by Monte Carlo from measured beam images, with an uncertainty set by simulation spread. [E2] - Precedent, as CODATA 2022 states it: LKB's 2020 experiment "uncovered previously unrecognized systematic effects in the earlier experiment and as a consequence" the 2020 h/m(Rb) is "fractionally smaller by about 3 parts in 10⁹" than LKB 2011. I have not checked which effects, or how much of the 3 ppb each accounts for. [E2] (My arithmetic: ≈1.5 ppb in α, 1.3× the present gap.) → Superseded in part by E018: the original authors told referees they had "no clear explanation" for this shift.

Inferences (probable but not entailed; confidence high/mod/low): - (moderate) Structurally, the beam-profile corrections resemble the E015 QED failure mode. Each is a modelled number with a small, internally derived error bar, large compared with the disagreement. A wrong model (e.g. missing small-scale wavefront structure) would not show up in the simulation-spread error bar. - (low–moderate) Which side is more exposed is not decidable from the budgets. Cs's beam correction is larger in absolute terms, which is what CODATA's "presumably" glosses over. Rb's is smaller but would have to be underestimated by more than 100%. - As first written this hour (22:3x PDT): "(low) The opposite printed signs (Cs −2.60, Rb +108.2) probably reflect different table conventions (effect vs correction), not opposite physics … Not verified." - Revised after reading Parker supplement §3 (parker.txt l.639–790), confidence raised to moderate: The opposite printed signs reflect conventions, not opposite physics. - Parker's Eq. S2 gives δk_eff/k_eff < 0, and the table lists that effect on α (−2.60 ppb). - Morel lists the correction (+108.2e-11). - In both experiments the beam correction therefore raises α (lowers α⁻¹), by 2.60 and 1.08 ppb. - Consequence for a common same-sign model error with fractional size f in both: the gap changes by f·(2.60−1.08) = f·1.52 ppb. f ≈ +77% (both overestimated) would close it, and f < 0 widens it. [E1 arithmetic on E2 inputs]

Hypotheses (testable; state the test): - H3 (T02): The Cs–Rb gap is dominated by an error in modelling wavefront/intensity structure (Gouy + "extra recoil") in at least one experiment. - Test: repeat each measurement with a substantially larger beam waist. The on-axis term scales as 1/w², so a beam-model error should shrink by roughly (w₁/w₂)², while a physics or other-systematic offset should not. Berkeley's rebuilt apparatus has a larger beam (E017). Disconfirmation: the new Cs value reproduces 137.035 999 046 within ~0.3 ppb at a larger w. - H1 (E002), restated (see Corrections): the next Cs result moves upward by ≥ 1×10⁻⁷ in α⁻¹ (≈ 0.7 ppb).

Theoretical explanations (candidate mechanisms/frameworks): - Bade et al. 2018 (PRL 121 073603): the local photon momentum is ħk_z with k_z = k[1 − (∇φ/k)²/2 + Δ⊥A/(2k²A)]. When atom survival depends on intensity (Bloch losses), the effective recoil is a survival-weighted average, k_eff = ⟨P k_z⟩/⟨P⟩. That average can exceed ħk ("extra recoil"). It depends on small-scale intensity structure that CCD imaging outside the vacuum chamber may not capture.

Speculation (clearly flagged; low evidence): - The "both experiments biased, in opposite directions" scenario from E015 would need two independent model errors. That is less parsimonious than one, but not excluded: g−2 sits between them (3.7σ / 2.5σ).
