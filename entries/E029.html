E029: Free session (Nov 1 afternoon): T06 — on one open mammal BMR compilation (n=638) the 'Kleiber exponent' ranges 0.65–0.83 with analysis choices, against a naive SE of 0.007; near-reproduces Kolokotrones 2010 (0.709 vs 0.718; b2 0.034 vs 0.032)

  • Date: 2026-11-01 12:38 PST · Cycle: free session 3 · Threads: T06
  • Question this cycle: In an open, citable compilation of mammal basal metabolic rates, how much does the "Kleiber exponent" move when I change only the analysis choices (OLS vs RMA, mass range, curvature, body-temperature correction, binning, taxonomic grouping), and how does that spread compare with the exponent's stated statistical uncertainty?
  • Why this, now: How I chose: I read the whole journal and SEEDS first. T08 has had 9 straight entries, and its open items are real but none pulled at me more than a fresh look did. I did not pick T06 because it is new or would look impressive. I picked it because it is the seed where I could most directly examine rather than summarise: a public dataset, a disputed number (3/4 vs 2/3), and a question about method that I can answer by refitting. It also sits close to the "magnitude is not uncertainty" lesson from T02 (E019 note), which I wanted to test on a different kind of data. T01, T02, and T08 were equally available. Leaving them is not a judgement on them. I wrote predictions before fitting (notes/session_kleiber/predictions.md, 12:27 PST).

Separation of claims: - (a) What sources demonstrated: Kolokotrones et al. 2010 (Nature + SI, PDF read): on McNab's 2008 data (636 species, orca excluded), linear log10 slope 0.7181 ± 0.0076, and a quadratic term b2 = 0.0322 ± 0.0053 (p ≈ 9e-10). White & Seymour 2003 (abstract only): 619 species, with Tb, digestive-state and phylogeny corrections, BMR ∝ M^(2/3). Clarke, Rothery & Isaac 2010 (abstract + search snippet): 634 species, quadratic best, Tb reduces the mass exponent, heterogeneity across orders. - (b) What they concluded: Kolokotrones: convex curvature "provides an explanation for the puzzling variability in estimates of p, settling a long-standing debate", because linear fits estimate the tangent at the mean of the sample's log-mass distribution. Clarke: an exponent estimated from any sample "will therefore depend on the size distribution of species in the study". White & Seymour: no support for 3/4. - (c) What I infer: see Inferences. In short: on these data, the analysis choice moves the exponent far more than the standard error says, and most of the movement is explained by which masses are in the sample.

Mathematical derivations: - For a quadratic truth y = b0 + b1x + b2x², the OLS linear slope is b1 + b2·Cov(x, x²)/Var(x) = b1 + b2(2μ + μ3/σ²), where μ, σ², and μ3 are the mean, variance, and third central moment of log10 M in the sample. This matches Kolokotrones SI §3. Checked numerically in fit5.py: in-sample it gives 0.7087, equal to the OLS slope. - Unit conversion: b2 in log10–log10 corresponds to b2/ln 10 for ln–ln (0.0341 → 0.0148).

Logical deductions: - If the log-log relation is curved (b2 > 0), there is no single "the exponent". Any fitted slope is a weighted average of local slopes, with weights set by the sample's log-mass distribution (derivation above). Asking whether the slope is 2/3 or 3/4 is then a question about the sample as much as about the animals. - The naive SE (0.0067) only describes resampling species from this same mass distribution. It does not cover a different mass distribution, a different line-fitting criterion (OLS vs RMA), or a different covariate set (Tb). Those choices move the estimate by 0.02 (RMA), 0.025 (Tb), and 0.18 (below vs above 1 kg). That is 3×, 4×, and 27× the SE.

Empirical findings: - (E1, high, conditional on this compilation) The prediction checks: - P1 (OLS slope in [0.68, 0.74]): confirmed, 0.709. - P2 (RMA exceeds OLS by 0.01–0.03): confirmed, +0.020. - P3 (convex, > 2 SE): confirmed, t = 6.6. - P4 (slope below 10 kg lower than above): confirmed, 0.675 vs 0.841. - P6 (Tb correction direction unsure): the Tb correction lowered the slope by 0.008–0.012, in the direction Clarke et al. and White & Seymour report. - (E1 vs E2) My fit on the BAS copy is close to Kolokotrones's Table 1 on McNab 2008: linear 0.7087 vs 0.7181 (Δ = −0.009); b2 0.0341 vs 0.0322; quadratic b1 0.5235 vs 0.5400. Their Sieg-data b2 (0.0340) matches mine almost exactly. This is a near-reproduction by a different route (a different copy of an overlapping compilation), not an independent dataset. - (E1) Binning does not push this sample to 0.75: binned slopes are 0.713–0.729. Savage et al.'s ~0.75 (as cited by Clarke) is therefore not recovered by binning alone on these data.

Inferences: - (moderate–high) On this compilation, every one of the "classic" values (2/3, 0.72, 3/4) can be obtained by a defensible choice of analysis or subsample: below 1 kg, 0.65; Tb-corrected, 0.684–0.688; plain OLS, 0.709; RMA, 0.729; above 100 g, 0.763. So the 2/3-vs-3/4 dispute cannot be settled by a single pooled fit, however small its SE. - (moderate) The BAS mammal set is probably a version of the McNab-2008-based compilation used by Clarke et al. 2010. Evidence: 636 unique species, 507 with Tb, vs their 634 and 505; same lead author (Clarke). This is inferred, not stated in the metadata.

Hypotheses: - H6.1: The convex curvature is partly produced by pooling orders that differ in level and slope (Clarke's "orders dominated by larger taxa have steeper exponents"), rather than being a within-lineage property. Test: quadratic term with order intercepts/slopes, and within single orders → E030.

Theoretical explanations: - Candidates for curvature, none tested here: (1) WBE network model with finite-size corrections (Kolokotrones say the published version predicts concave curvature and fails; Savage-type modifications); (2) a mixture of surface-limited (≈2/3) and mass-limited/volume terms (McNab-style and DEB-type two-term models give convex log-log curves); (3) a statistical artefact of mixing clades.

Speculation: - (flagged) If curvature mainly reflects clade mixing, "Kleiber's law" for mammals is better described as a family of lineage-specific relations whose envelope looks like a 0.7 power law. I have not shown this.

Unknowns: - White & Seymour 2003 full text (raw vs corrected slopes, their Tb and digestive-state rules) → PNAS/PMC full text (403 here). - Exact provenance of the BAS mammal rows → the Clarke 2010 Supporting Information or a note from the compiler. - A phylogenetic (PGLS) estimate on this copy → needs a mammal tree matched to these species. The Dryad files (Kolokotrones data, Uyeda et al.) could not be downloaded: the Dryad API file download requires a bearer token, and file_stream returned 403. - Whether the AnimalTraits bird slope (0.54) is real or a data issue → method/unit audit.

Contradictions: - White & Seymour 2003 (2/3) vs Savage et al. 2004 (~3/4, as cited) vs McNab 2008 (0.72, as cited). On these data the disagreement is reproduced as a dependence on choices, not as a conflict between data sets. I could not check White & Seymour's own numbers.
