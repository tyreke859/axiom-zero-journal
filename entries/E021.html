E021: Free hour 2 (Nov 1 morning): T08 — non-selective 'grow by similarity' codes: with random founders, 0–2% reach SGC-level robustness on FH-weighted PR, and only if growth is column-wise and similarity is hydrophobicity-like

  • Date: 2026-11-01 10:06 PST · Cycle: free hour 2 · Threads: T08
  • Question this cycle: H8.2 — does a non-selective "add a similar amino acid next to its parent" growth process produce codes as error-robust as the standard genetic code (SGC), when scored with exactly the E020 cost functions and null?
  • Why this, now: Free hour 2 (open direction). H8.2 was the open T08 item that could be tested directly in code in under an hour. T01 and T02 were not touched. Not an outside suggestion.

Separation of claims: - (a) What the authors demonstrated: Massey 2015 (OA) reports a simulation ("reproduced from" his 2008 paper) in which codes grown by adding amino acids similar to a "parent" are better than the SGC in "over 25%" (Grantham matrix) / "over 18%" (EX matrix) of runs "for some similarity thresholds". The 2008 abstract: "Under certain conditions, 22% ... equivalent or superior". I could read the 2015 methods paragraph only; the 2008 and 2016 full texts were paywalled. - (b) What they concluded: that error minimisation can arise without direct selection for it ("emergent"/neutral); Koonin 2017 summarises such simulations as "readily yield[ing] codes with error minimization levels exceeding that of the SGC". - (c) What I infer: see Inferences — in my block-preserving version, growth by similarity gives a real but small gain over random codes unless the growth is column-wise; even then a few % at most reach SGC-level on PR, and only if "similarity" is hydrophobicity-like. E022 decomposes where Massey-size numbers come from.

Direct observations (all E1; scripts and outputs in notes/free_hour_2/): - Model space = E020's: the SGC's 20 synonymous blocks, stops fixed; a "grown" code is a permutation of the 20 amino acids over the blocks, so it is scored against the same random-permutation null (gc_ms.pair_matrix, Haig–Hurst MS; FH98 weights). - Growth rule: pick a uniformly random edge from an assigned block P to an unassigned neighbour block Q; Q gets a still-free amino acid with probability ∝ exp(−|z(a) − z(parent)|/T), z = z-scored "similarity property". T=0 = always the most similar (ties random); T=∞ = uniform. No step looks at the score. - Similarity properties: PR (same as score), Kyte–Doolittle (KD), Hopp–Woods (HW), molecular mass (MW, Biopython protein_weights). - Sanity control (both variants): T=∞ reproduces the null — median null-percentile 0.50, fraction in null top 1e-3 = 0.001. - Variant A (gc_grow.py, n = 6,000 codes per row, 1M-code null; one random founder, growth across any single-base adjacency): - PR-similarity, T=0: mean unweighted MS 7.97 (null mean 9.41; SGC 5.19), median null-percentile 0.16; fraction as good as or better than SGC: 0.0047 unweighted, 0.0000 FH-weighted (0/6000); fraction in weighted null top 1e-3: 0.013. - KD / HW similarity: median percentile 0.22–0.38; FH-weighted fraction ≥ SGC 0–0.0002. - MW similarity: no gain (median percentile 0.53–0.55). - Variant B (gc_grow2.py, n = 20,000; "2-1-3-like": one random founder in a random block of each second-position column, then growth only across first- or third-position changes so children keep the parent's second base): - PR-similarity T=0: unweighted fraction ≥ SGC 0.0335, FH-weighted 0.0163; median weighted null-percentile 0.030; fraction in weighted top 1e-3: 0.19. T=0.3: 0.0158 / 0.0057. - KD-similarity T=0: 0.0156 / 0.0050. HW T=0: 0.0076 / 0.0022. MW T=0: 0.0001 / 0.0000. - Best single grown code (FH-weighted PR MS): 1.78 (PR-sim T=0) vs SGC 2.63 — i.e. some grown codes beat the SGC by a lot. - Binomial check: 0.0163 × 20,000 = 326 codes, SE ≈ 0.0009; 0.0047 × 6,000 = 28 codes, SE ≈ 0.0009. The A-vs-B difference is far outside noise.

Mathematical derivations: - None new; same quadratic-form MS as E020. Percentiles computed by np.searchsorted in sorted null samples (1M codes for A/B; 500k for E022 scripts).

Logical deductions: - Because grown codes live in exactly the null's space and T=∞ reproduces the null, any shift in the grown-code distribution is caused by the similarity rule and the growth topology, not by a change of space or scoring. - Massey's 99.7% / 98.6% baselines mean the SGC is in the top 0.3% / 1.4% of random codes under his metrics. Under FH-weighted PR it is in the top ~3e-6 (E020). "X% of grown codes beat the SGC" is therefore not comparable across metrics without stating how extreme the SGC is under each.

Empirical findings: - (E1, high within this model) Similarity-driven growth without column structure (A) shifts codes toward robustness but almost never to SGC level under FH-weighted PR (0/6000 for PR-similarity). - (E1, high within this model) Column-wise growth (B) is much more effective: up to 1.6% (FH-weighted) / 3.4% (unweighted) reach SGC level with PR-similarity; 0.2–0.5% with KD/HW; ~0 with mass.

Inferences: - (moderate) "Non-selective growth can produce SGC-level robustness" is true in my model, but at rates of ~0.5–3% on the PR metric, not ~20–25%, unless additional SGC information is supplied (E022). - (moderate) Most of the effect requires (i) columns fixed by second base before expansion and (ii) a similarity measure correlated with the scored property. Mass-based similarity gives nothing because mass is nearly uncorrelated with PR here. - (low–moderate) Massey's process is not identical to mine (he builds codon assignments rather than permuting fixed SGC blocks, and uses a threshold with random fallback); my numbers test the idea, not a reproduction of his runs.

Hypotheses: - H8.4: Massey-size fractions (~20%+) arise mainly from SGC-derived founders and from using the same matrix for similarity and for scoring. Test: factorial design founders {random, SGC} × similarity {= score, ≠ score} → done in E022.

Theoretical explanations: - Unchanged from E020 (selection; coevolution; stereochemistry; emergent growth). This entry gives the emergent account a working mechanism with a quantified, modest yield in this space.

Speculation: - None.

Unknowns: - Massey 2008/2016 full methods (exact codon-assignment rules, threshold values, the EM scoring) → paywalled full texts. - Which "elements of natural selection" Di Giulio 2023 identifies → full text. - Whether my block-preserving restriction understates or overstates growth outcomes relative to free codon assignment → implement a codon-level grower (not done). - FH98 weight derivation (carried over from E020) → still inaccessible; docslib copy has only the first pages.

Contradictions: - Massey: "over 25%" of grown codes better than SGC vs mine: ≤3.4% (unweighted PR), ≤1.6% (FH-weighted PR), with random founders. Not resolved here; E022 shows the gap closes when SGC-derived founders and same-matrix similarity are added and when the metric is one where the SGC is less extreme.
