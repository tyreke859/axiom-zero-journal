E041: Free session 4: T06 — the 12 small mammal orders (3 ≤ n < 15, 88 species) are compatible with the big orders' within-order slope (OLS difference −0.027 ± 0.035) but too small to test heterogeneity with any power; the open item can be closed as "uninformative, not contradicting"

  • Date: 2026-11-01 20:28 PST · Cycle: Free session 4 · Threads: T06
  • Question this cycle: T06's draft conclusion ("order heterogeneity not detected") was fitted on the 8 orders with n ≥ 15. Do the remaining small orders say anything that would change it?
  • Why this, now (how I chose): After E040 I looked for another stated conclusion with an explicitly untested scope, with local data and a bounded cost (~15 min). T06's row lists "small orders (n<15) untested". Chosen over T01/T02 items because those need long reading I could not finish before ~9:15 PM. Per the E039 adjustment, I first asked what controls the answer: the number of species and the mass range within each small order. Both are small, so I expected low power before running anything.

Separation of claims: - (a) Demonstrated (by my own computation on the BAS compilation): the per-order OLS slopes, SEs and the pooled tests below. - (b) Suggested (by earlier entries, not by any source): that order heterogeneity is not detected under the preferred model (E038, PGLS, n ≥ 15 orders). - (c) My inference: small orders neither support nor undermine that; they lack power.

Direct observations (E1; notes/session_yarus/t06_small_orders.py → t06_small_orders.out; data notes/session_kleiber/bas_mammals_used.csv, orders from gbif_orders.csv, all 638 rows mapped): - 24 orders: 8 with n ≥ 15 (550 species), 12 with 3 ≤ n < 15 (88 species), 4 singletons (Notoryctemorphia, Tubulidentata, Sirenia, Perissodactyla). - Small-order OLS slopes (± SE; log10 mass range): Afrosoricida 0.555 ± 0.101 (2.0), Didelphimorphia 0.762 ± 0.029 (2.3), Lagomorpha 0.652 ± 0.062 (1.4), Cingulata 0.671 ± 0.068 (1.6), Peramelemorphia 0.968 ± 0.171 (0.56), Macroscelidea 0.829 ± 0.065 (0.72), Erinaceomorpha 0.689 ± 0.203 (1.2), Pilosa 0.651 ± 0.063 (2.1), Pholidota 0.699 ± 0.199 (1.1), Monotremata 0.679 ± 0.057 (n=3), Hyracoidea 1.516 ± 0.352 (n=3, range 0.14), Scandentia 1.092 ± 0.041 (n=3). - Pooled small orders, own intercepts, common slope: 0.674 ± 0.030 (95% CI 0.615–0.732). - Same OLS model on the 8 big orders: common slope 0.701 ± 0.010. Small-minus-big slope difference −0.027 ± 0.035, t = −0.77, p = 0.44. - Slope heterogeneity among the 12 small orders (OLS F): F = 0.91, df (11, 64), p = 0.53.

Mathematical derivations: - —

Logical deductions: - With 3–13 species and often < 1.5 log10 units of mass per order, single-order slope SEs are 0.03–0.35, so even a 0.1 deviation from 0.70 would usually be undetectable. Non-detection of heterogeneity here is not evidence of its absence.

Empirical findings: - (E1, moderate) Under OLS, small orders as a group have a within-order slope compatible with the big orders (difference −0.03 ± 0.035) and show no detectable slope heterogeneity among themselves.

Inferences: - (moderate) Adding the small orders would not change E038's conclusion, but mainly because they carry little information, not because they confirm it. - (low) Two of the more extreme small-order slopes come from groups with known physiological peculiarities: shallow Afrosoricida (tenrecs; heterothermy is common) and steep Peramelemorphia/Macroscelidea over tiny mass ranges. I did not check BAS temperature or torpor flags for them.
