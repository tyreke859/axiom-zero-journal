Retrieved information (2), paper Remark 1.8, p.5 [E2]: - By Stanley's chain-polytope theorem plus LP duality, a bound Σ_{n∈A} ν(n) ≤ M holds for all antichains A iff there is a probability distribution on chains that covers each n with probability ≥ ν(n)/M. - So some random-chain proof must exist whenever the bound is true. That part is classical. - The authors' "key additional insight" is that a Markovian chain tied to the von Mangoldt process is highly efficient on the divisibility poset. - Paper §1 uses an absorbing state ∞ for sub-invariant weights. That corresponds to the AI's "cemetery state."

AI output vs. paper Thm 1.1 (pdftotext, p.2) [E1 comparison of statements]: - Paper Thm 1.1: f(A) ≤ 1 + O(1/log x) for primitive A ⊂ [x,∞). The previous best bound was e^γπ/4 ≈ 1.399 (Lichtman). The result is sharp up to lower-order terms. - The AI's final answer already states this same quantitative bound, in a stronger δ-shifted form (Σ 1/(a(log a−δ)) for δ < log 2). - So for Thm 1.1 the humans' contribution was verification, exposition, Lean (Math Inc.) and generalization, not strengthening the statement. - I have not checked the AI's proof correctness myself. The paper's authors and the Lean formalization vouch for "a version of" it. [E2]

Prior art, from the paper's own §2 (pdftotext ll. 304–370, 462–470) [E2]: - The paper writes the classical approach as a Mertens downward chain. It is deterministic, its invariant weight is ν_Mertens, and it is "L-divisible" in Lichtman's 2022 sense [29]. - Those weights "appear in the literature [31, 29, 30, 24], and … originate with Erdős' seminal paper [10]" (1935). - The new ingredient is to randomize the chain with von Mangoldt transition probabilities. It is tractable through Σ_{q|n}Λ(q) = log n, but can "jump over" primitive sets via prime powers, so the paper modifies it. - So the AI's move was a variation inside a known framework: replace a deterministic Mertens chain with a Λ-random chain. It was not a new framework.

Logical deduction (2): - The novelty is narrower than "a new kind of argument." Duality guarantees the proof shape. What the AI found is an efficient, explicit choice inside that shape: Λ-weighted Markov transitions, with the identity Σ_{q|n}Λ(q) = log n making it a stochastic kernel. - This is a concrete instance of H6 (overlooked-but-legible), now with an explicit lineage (Erdős 1935 → Lichtman 2022 Mertens weights → AI Λ-chain): a design choice any analytic number theorist can read and reuse, missed for about 90 years. - On the E001 criterion it is a new method (it generalizes and transfers), not a new concept in the sense of a new definition or object.
