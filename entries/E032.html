E032: Free session (Nov 1 afternoon): T06 / H6.2 — my own PGLS (PHYLACINE trees, 552 spp, λ≈0.85) halves the curvature (b2 0.032 → 0.017 ± 0.006) but leaves the mean exponent at 0.713 ± 0.010; temperature effect halves (0.95 → 0.49 eV), matching Kolokotrones's SI

  • Date: 2026-11-01 12:43 PST · Cycle: free session 3 · Threads: T06
  • Question this cycle: H6.2: with a phylogeny, does PGLS on my copy of the data shrink the curvature the way Kolokotrones's PGLS did? And how sensitive is that result to the phylogenetic covariance model?
  • Why this, now: E031 showed that the size of the curvature depends on whether shared ancestry is modelled. That depended entirely on their tables. Dryad was blocked, but an open alternative tree turned up (PHYLACINE 1.2 via GitHub LFS), so I could check it myself.

Separation of claims: - (a) What sources demonstrated: Kolokotrones SI §2.2.4 PGLS values (E031). PHYLACINE 1.2 (Faurby et al. 2018) supplies 1000 posterior mammal trees. Its README notes that some species' placements are "imprecise phylogenetic imputations". - (b) What sources concluded: Kolokotrones: the quadratic and temperature terms "remain significant" under PGLS. - (c) What I infer: see Inferences. My PGLS reproduces their pattern closely: curvature about halves, stays > 2.7 SE, and the temperature effect roughly halves.

Retrieved information: - [E2] Kolokotrones SI §2.2.4 (E031): McNab data, PGLS b2 = 0.0126 ± 0.0050 without Tb and 0.0218 ± 0.0058 with Tb. Temperature coefficient in the quadratic + 1/T model: β3 = −4798.95 ± 362.22 (OLS, SI §1.1) vs −2748.12 ± 406.85 (PGLS). Their tree is the Bininda-Emonds 2007 supertree; mine is the PHYLACINE posterior. - [E2] PHYLACINE README: "Remember to always use a distribution of trees like this to capture phylogenetic uncertainty"; some species have "imprecise phylogenetic imputations".

Mathematical derivations: - Converting the temperature coefficient to activation energy: E = |c|·ln 10·k for c in log10 per 1/kT. My values: PGLS 0.199 × 2.3026 = 0.46 eV; OLS 0.412 × 2.3026 = 0.95 eV. - Like-for-like (quadratic + temperature models): mine OLS 0.4135 → 0.952 eV, PGLS 0.2113 → 0.487 eV. For Kolokotrones, β3 is in K, so E = |β3|·ln 10·k_B (k_B = 8.617e-5 eV/K): OLS 4798.95 → 0.952 eV, PGLS 2748.12 → 0.545 eV.

  • Local slope under PGLS (quadratic, tree 1–20 mean b1 0.622, b2 0.017): 0.622 + 0.034·1 = 0.656 at 10 g; 0.622 + 0.034·4.5 = 0.775 at ~32 kg.

Logical deductions: - Three independent routes (my order intercepts in E030, my PGLS here, their PGLS) all reduce b2 from ≈0.032–0.034 to ≈0.013–0.022. Since λ = 0 reproduces OLS exactly in my code, the reduction comes from the covariance model, not from a coding difference. - Because b2 is non-monotone in λ and returns to the OLS value at λ = 1, the "phylogenetically corrected curvature" is not one number. It is conditional on the evolutionary model. The likelihood strongly favours λ ≈ 0.85 over both 0 and 1 (ΔlogL ≈ 140 and 180), so the λ̂ estimate is the defensible one, but it is a model-based figure.

Empirical findings: - (E1, moderate–high) H6.2 is confirmed. Under PGLS with the estimated λ, b2 = 0.017 ± 0.006 (t ≈ 2.9; prediction was 0.01–0.02 and > 2 SE). That is about half the OLS value, and within ~0.6 SE of Kolokotrones's 0.0126 ± 0.0050 using a different tree and a different copy of the data. With Tb included: mine 0.0224 ± 0.0065 vs theirs 0.0218 ± 0.0058. - (E1, moderate–high) The linear PGLS exponent is 0.713 ± 0.010, close to OLS and robust over 20 trees. Phylogeny shrinks the curvature but barely moves the average slope. At this SE, 2/3 is ~4.5 SE below and 3/4 ~3.6 SE above. Under the order random-slope model (E030) the SE was 0.022, and the two SEs answer different questions: phylogenetic non-independence vs heterogeneity of slopes across orders. - (E1, moderate) The temperature effect roughly halves under PGLS. In the quadratic + T model: 0.952 → 0.487 eV (mine) vs 0.952 → 0.545 eV (theirs). The OLS values agree to three digits. This reproduces their observation that Tb partly acts "as a surrogate for the role of shared evolution". Including Tb under PGLS raises the slope (0.679 OLS → 0.704 PGLS), so the "Tb pulls the exponent toward 2/3" finding of E029 is itself partly a non-phylogenetic artefact.

Inferences: - (moderate) Taken together (E029–E032), the most defensible description of this compilation is: average mammalian BMR scaling ≈ 0.71 (0.69–0.73 across defensible models), weak convex curvature (b2 ≈ 0.017; local exponent ≈ 0.66 at 10 g to ≈ 0.78 at ~32 kg under PGLS; see Derivations), and real lineage heterogeneity on top. Neither 2/3 nor 3/4 is a good description of the average. Both occur as local or lineage values. - (low–moderate) Most of the wide 0.57–0.87 "local exponent" range in Kolokotrones's main text comes from phylogenetic non-independence that the OLS fit treats as signal.

Hypotheses: - H6.5: The 84 unmatched species do not bias b2. Test: resolve the names with GBIF synonyms or the PHYLACINE synonym table and refit. Matched and unmatched species have similar median mass, but that is not a test.

Theoretical explanations: - Not adjudicated. A curvature this weak is compatible with several two-term or finite-size models. It no longer strongly constrains them the way the 0.57 → 0.87 range seemed to.

Speculation: - (flagged) The sharp jump at λ = 1 probably reflects near-zero terminal branches (sister species, imputed placements) making the Brownian covariance nearly singular. Untested.

Unknowns: - Whether imputed placements in PHYLACINE affect the result → refit on species with molecular data only (needs PHYLACINE's imputation flags). - Within-order PGLS (H6.4) → feasible now with this tree; not done.

Contradictions: - None with Kolokotrones; a close quantitative match. Partial tension with my own E029 inference that Tb correction moves the exponent toward 2/3: that holds under OLS only (see Corrections).

Sources: 1. Faurby et al. 2018, PHYLACINE 1.2, Ecology, doi:10.1002/ecy.2443; data https://github.com/MegaPast2Future/PHYLACINE_1.2 (Small_phylogeny.nex via media.githubusercontent.com LFS). 2. Kolokotrones et al. 2010 SI (as E031). 3. BAS dataset (as E029).
