E017: Free hour (Oct 31 night): T02 — what happened after 2020: LKB now maps the photon recoil in situ and finds ppb-scale local k-vector deviations a Gaussian model misses; Berkeley rebuilt with a larger beam; no new α value from either

  • Date: 2026-10-31 22:25 PDT · Cycle: Free hour · Threads: T02
  • Question this cycle: Since 2020, has either group found or ruled out a hidden beam-related bias, or published a new value?
  • Why this, now: This follows from E016, my own choice within the outside-suggested T02 focus. The largest correction on both sides is a beam-model term, so I checked what later work says about exactly that term.

Separation of claims (added per Reke's directive refinement, 22:27 PDT): - (a) What the authors demonstrated: - Gaudout et al. measured position-resolved recoil with a BEC. Local κ variations exceed the ~0.2 ppb a Gaussian model predicts. - The grid average agrees with the molasses value. - With a clipped beam, κ_z reverses sign, and their Monte Carlo reproduces it from an in-situ intensity map. - (b) What their findings suggest (their words): "significant wavefront distortions beyond those expected from a simple Gaussian model". The LKB SPIE abstract says the discrepancy is "most likely due to systematic effects that are either misestimated or not yet identified". Neither group says that Morel 2020 or Parker 2018 is wrong, or by how much. - (c) What I independently infer: - That this makes beam-model corrections built from ex-situ images more uncertain than their quoted error bars might indicate. This is my reading. Gaudout et al. did not apply their map to the 2020 configuration, and the averaged agreement they report cuts against a large averaged bias in their current setup. - Checked by me: the 0.2 ppb Gaussian variation. - Only retrieved: everything else in this entry, including "no new α value" (based on searches and slides, so absence-of-evidence).

Direct observations (what I computed or ran myself this cycle; script path + output): - Read the Gaudout 2025 text (notes/free_hour/gaudout2025.txt, l.225–330) and both MITP 2025 slide decks (roth_mitp2025.txt, guellati_mitp2025.txt). [E1 reading] - My recomputation of the pure-Gaussian variation (Gaudout's κ_z = −(2/k²w²)(1 − r²/w²), at w = 5 mm, r = 0–2 mm) gives 1.23 ppb × (2/5)² ≈ 0.2 ppb. This matches their stated "of the order of 2×10⁻¹⁰". [E1]

Retrieved information (what sources say; quote numbers exactly; [E2/E3/E4] + URL): - Gaudout, Si-Ahmed, Debavelaere, Door, Cladé, Guellati-Khelifa (arXiv 2507.19157, 2025): - They use a BEC (RMS size 350 µm) moved across a 5 mm-waist beam as an in-situ probe of the local recoil, with a 121-point 2D grid and 117 h of data. - Per-point uncertainty is 6×10⁻¹⁰ on κ. - They find spatial variations of κ "negligible compared to the much larger deviations observed experimentally". From the Fig. 2 axis range this is ppb-scale; I read it off the plot only (low precision). - "This suggests the presence of significant wavefront distortions beyond those expected from a simple Gaussian model." - But "the average value of these measurements is in good agreement with the values obtained using an optical molasses." - With a deliberately clipped beam, they observe a local "extra recoil" exceeding hν/c and a sign reversal of κ_z. Their Monte Carlo reproduces it from the measured intensity map. [E2] - Guellati-Khelifa, MITP 2025 slides: - "Our strategy to solve 5σ discrepancy: new method to probe in-situ the spatial laser beam profile → better evaluation of the wave vectors?" - "New measurement using AI based Bragg diffraction (Berkeley scheme)" is planned, as a test of beam-splitter-method systematics. - They see temporal fluctuations with the BEC from a parasitic interference in the new optical setup. - Statistical uncertainty is "limited by the time required to scan the full beam profile". - No new h/m value is shown. [E2] - LKB SPIE 2026 abstract (https://doi.org/10.1117/12.3089685): the discrepancy is "most likely due to systematic effects that are either misestimated or not yet identified". [E2, abstract only] - Roth (Berkeley), MITP 2025 slides: - A new apparatus: "new chamber, new simulation, new team", 4 m interferometer region, larger beam. - The larger beam is meant to give "reduced effect of thermal motion, Gouy phase, laser phase"; baffles to reduce speckle. - "One unified simulation for understanding all beam related systematics", being verified stage by stage against single-Bragg-pulse data. - "Experiment can now run 24/7." No new α value. [E2] - DAMOP 2025 abstracts on the same apparatus: https://meetings-archive.aps.org/damop/2025/h00/152/ · https://meetings-archive.aps.org/damop/2025/n09/3/ [E2] - Ironically, a speculative "IR/UV mixing" paper (arXiv 2508.06171) fits the Cs/Rb/g−2 spread as new physics. Its quoted inputs match mine: 137.035999160(14), 045(27), 2052(97). [E2; physics claim not assessed, E4-level for this thread]

Mathematical derivations (steps shown or script path): - See Direct observations: the Gaussian Gouy term varies by only ~0.2 ppb over the r = 0–2 mm cloud region at w = 5 mm.

Logical deductions (follows necessarily from the above): - As first written: "So any analysis that relies on the Gaussian model plus CCD-imaged intensity outside the chamber is model-dependent at a level comparable to the gap." Reclassified 22:3x PDT: that sentence is an inference (low–moderate), not a deduction. The paper shows large local deviations, not a gap-sized error in a cloud-averaged correction. What does follow necessarily is narrower: a pure Gaussian model does not describe the local recoil in LKB's current beam. - It does not show that Morel 2020 is biased. The cloud-averaged (molasses) value agreed with the BEC grid average. The relevant question is whether 2020's Monte Carlo got the average right, and this result neither confirms nor refutes that for the 2020 beam.

Empirical findings (established by data/experiment, mine or published; with evidence grade): - Neither group has published a new α since 2020 (Rb) and 2018 (Cs), as of 2026-10-31 (arXiv, OpenAlex, MITP 2025 talks, SPIE 2026). [E2, absence-of-evidence with fair coverage] - Both groups' current programmes target beam/wavefront systematics specifically. [E2]

Inferences (probable but not entailed; confidence high/mod/low): - (moderate) LKB's SPIE 2026 abstract names misestimated or unidentified systematics as "most likely". In what I read, I found no equivalent explicit statement from Berkeley; their slides describe upgrades aimed at beam-related systematics. Earlier wording this hour said "the groups themselves", which over-attributed LKB's statement to both groups. - (low–moderate) LKB's switch to Bragg diffraction ("Berkeley scheme") would remove the Raman-vs-Bragg difference as a discriminator, but it also tests one candidate class directly.

Hypotheses (testable; state the test): - H3 (E016) has a natural test coming: Berkeley's larger-beam apparatus. If the new Cs value moves toward Rb/g−2 by roughly the fraction of the beam correction that 1/w² scaling removes, that supports H3. If it stays at 046 within its error, H3 is weakened for Cs. - H4 (T02): LKB's in-situ map, applied to the beam configuration actually used in 2020 (if it is reconstructable), would shift h/m(Rb) by < 0.3 ppb. Test: any LKB re-analysis or new value. Disconfirmation: a revision ≥ 0.5 ppb.

Theoretical explanations (candidate mechanisms/frameworks): - Bade 2018 extra recoil (survival-weighted Laplacian term), parasitic interferometers aliasing with chosen pulse times (Parker 2016, up to ~1 ppb), and Bragg "anomalous phase" (Roth slides). All three are beam/diffraction-model effects.

Speculation (clearly flagged; low evidence): - New-physics fits (e.g. arXiv 2508.06171) need the Cs/Rb difference to be real, i.e. species-dependent at the 1 ppb level. Nothing I read rules this out, but the existence of three uncharacterised beam-model channels each capable of ~1 ppb makes it the less economical reading for now. This is not a finding.

Unknowns (what I could not determine → what evidence would settle it): - When either group will publish a new value → their next papers. Nothing is announced with a date. - Whether Morel 2020's beam configuration can be retro-mapped → only LKB can say. - Exact ppb scale of the Gaudout Fig. 2 local deviations → the figure data or a higher-resolution read.

Contradictions (between sources, or with my results or earlier entries): - None found between sources. CODATA's "presumably" (that Berkeley had already reduced the LKB-2011 effects) is an assumption, not a demonstration (E016).
