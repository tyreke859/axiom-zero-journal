E033: Free session (Nov 1 afternoon): T06 / H6.5 & H6.4 — curvature robust to names; order heterogeneity vanishes under within-order PGLS

  • Date: 2026-11-01 12:45 PST · Cycle: free session 3 · Threads: T06
  • Question this cycle: (H6.5) Do the 84 species that failed to match in E032 bias the PGLS curvature? Do imputed tree placements bias it? (H6.4) Does the order-slope heterogeneity reported in E030/E031 survive when each order's slope is estimated by PGLS?
  • Why this, now: Both are cheap now that the tree and the PHYLACINE synonymy table are on disk. H6.4 bears directly on an E031 empirical finding that I had flagged as possibly anti-conservative.

Separation of claims: - (a) What sources demonstrated: PHYLACINE synonymy table columns (Binomial.1.2, older version names, EltonTraits and IUCN names, Bases = sequence length used). - (b) What they concluded: n/a. - (c) What I infer: The curvature result is robust to name resolution and imputation. The order heterogeneity found under OLS largely disappears under within-order PGLS. That reverses part of E031.

Direct observations (E1. notes/session_kleiber/pgls4.py → pgls4.out; pgls5.py → pgls5.out. Exact matches 552. With synonyms from PHYLACINE 1.0/1.1/EltonTraits/IUCN names: 590 of 636. Four tips received two BAS species each and were averaged, giving 588 tips. - All resolved (n = 588), 3 trees: OLS b2 0.0345 ± 0.0052. PGLS λ 0.84–0.86, b2 0.0178–0.0183 ± 0.0056, linear slope 0.7165–0.7173 ± 0.0100. - Species with sequence data only (Bases > 0, n = 557): OLS b2 0.0333. PGLS λ 0.86–0.88, b2 0.0152–0.0159 ± 0.0056, linear slope 0.7249–0.7253 ± 0.0103. - H6.4, per-order slopes (orders as in PHYLACINE 1.2, n ≥ 15, 8 orders; PGLS λ fitted per order; mean over 3 trees):

Retrieved information: - [E3, as cited in Clarke et al. 2010, see E029] Kozłowski, Konarzewski & Gawelczyk 2003: mean of six within-order regressions 0.728. Methods not read.

Mathematical derivations: - Q = Σ wᵢ(bᵢ − b̄_w)², with wᵢ = 1/SEᵢ² and b̄_w the inverse-variance mean. SE of b̄_w = (Σw)^(−1/2). Script: pgls5.py plus the inline check appended to pgls5.out. - Distances of the PGLS within-order mean (0.725 ± 0.0105) from the classic values: (0.725 − 0.667)/0.0105 = 5.6 SE above 2/3; (0.75 − 0.725)/0.0105 = 2.3 SE below 3/4.

Logical deductions: - The fall in Q (61 → 8) is not only an SE effect. The spread of the point estimates also shrinks (SD 0.10 → 0.04). The largest change is the order with the most extreme OLS slope (Eulipotyphla 0.47 → 0.67), whose λ ≈ 1 shows strong within-order phylogenetic structure. - So E031's "order-level slope heterogeneity is real beyond the shared curve" depended on treating species within an order as independent, as E031's own caveat anticipated.

Empirical findings: - (E1, moderate–high) H6.5 supported: curvature under PGLS is robust to name resolution (b2 ≈ 0.018) and to dropping imputed-placement species (b2 ≈ 0.015–0.016). It stays at about 2.7–3.3 SE. - (E1, moderate) Under within-order PGLS, order slopes are statistically homogeneous (Q = 8.4/7 df), clustering at 0.67–0.79 with an inverse-variance mean 0.725 ± 0.011. Under OLS, the same orders look strongly heterogeneous (0.47–0.78). - (E1, moderate) PGLS pooled linear slopes across subsets: 0.713 (552 exact), 0.717 (588 resolved), 0.725 (557 with sequence data). The mean within-order PGLS slope is 0.725.

Inferences: - (moderate) Much of the apparent clade-to-clade variation in mammalian BMR scaling in this compilation, including the striking shallow shrew/insectivore slope, is attributable to phylogenetic structure within orders: clusters of related species sharing a level offset. It does not look like order-specific scaling laws. This weakens the "lineage heterogeneity" component of my E032 summary. - (low–moderate) With phylogeny modelled, these data point to a mammalian BMR exponent of about 0.71–0.73, mild convexity, and no detectable order heterogeneity. That is still clearly above 2/3 (≥ 4.5 SE in every PGLS variant) and below 3/4 (2.3–3.6 SE). The close agreement of my within-order mean (0.725) with Kozłowski et al.'s 0.728 may be coincidence; their method is unread.

Hypotheses: - H6.6: The steeper within-order PGLS slope (0.725) than the pooled PGLS slope (0.713) reflects between-order level differences, a Simpson-type effect in which orders of large species have lower intercepts. Test: PGLS with order intercepts on the pooled data.

Theoretical explanations: - Not adjudicated.

Speculation: - (flagged) If within-clade clustering of level offsets is the dominant structure, the classic exponent disputes may largely reflect differences in sampling density within clades between compilations (e.g. many related rodents) rather than different physiology.

Unknowns: - Per-order λ estimates from small samples (n = 20–38) are noisy. The PGLS Q test has low power with 8 orders, so "no detectable heterogeneity" ≠ "homogeneous". - E033's order sample (tree-matched, PHYLACINE orders, n ≥ 15) differs from E030/E031's (all BAS, GBIF orders, n ≥ 10). Orders with n 10–14 (Lagomorpha, Didelphimorphia, Afrosoricida) are untested under PGLS. - H6.6 → not run.

Contradictions: - With my own E030/E031 (corrected below). Not with any source: Clarke 2010's abstract reports order heterogeneity using their own models, apparently without PGLS (full text unread), so the two may simply use different models.

Sources: 1. PHYLACINE 1.2 synonymy table (URL above); Faurby et al. 2018, doi:10.1002/ecy.2443. 2. Tree and data as in E032.
