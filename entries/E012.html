E012: Cycle 0 (pre-work, cont.): Erdős #1051 (irrationality of Σ1/(aₙaₙ₊₁)) — loose AI proof, humans sharpened it to the optimal golden-ratio threshold

  • Date: 2026-10-31 19:39–19:42 PDT · Cycle: 0 (pre-work) · Threads: T01
  • Question this cycle: For the next 1(a) full solution (#1051, Aletheia, Jan 2026), what did the AI contribute and what did the humans contribute?

Direct observations: (from arXiv:2601.21442v3, Barreto–Kang–Kim–Kovač–Zhang, at data/bkkkz_1051.pdf, §1 "Declaration of AI usage") [E2] - Problem (Erdős–Graham 1980): if liminf a_n^{1/2^n} > 1, is Σ 1/(a_n a_{n+1}) irrational? - Timeline: - (2) Aletheia found a solution. "the proof used rough estimates such as 1.7 < 2, log x < x." - (3) "The authors realized and proved that one can relax the original growth condition" - (4) "Gemini Deep Think discovered a further generalization … and then, jointly with Aletheia, proved it" - (5) The humans found tight counterexamples. - (6) The humans weakened the hypotheses further, "inspired by Erdős's paper [5]" - Final result: irrational if limsup a_n^{1/φ^n} = ∞, with φ the golden ratio. This is essentially optimal: for every C there are rational examples with lim a_n^{1/φ^n} = C. - Aletheia "searched over a large set of open problems and autonomously narrowed them down to those it could solve." - The main proof case uses "variations of the strategy from [5]" (Erdős's own paper).

Logical deductions: - The AI proof is a ∀-proof using Erdős's classical strategy with slack, so it is a connection/application, not a method. - The deeper result (the golden-ratio threshold and its optimality) is mostly human, with one AI-proposed generalization (step 4). - Pattern match with #1196 (E009): in both, an AI output with visible slack or an unusual device was sharpened or abstracted by humans into a stronger, more general theorem. - The difference: in #1051 the device was Erdős's own, so there is no new method.

Inferences: - (moderate) A recurring 2026 workflow: an AI triages obscure problems and produces a loose proof, then humans abstract or sharpen it. In both well-documented cases (#1196, #1051) the conceptual payoff came in the human phase. - Aletheia's self-selection of problems it "could solve" is a selection effect that supports H4 (obscurity, not difficulty).
