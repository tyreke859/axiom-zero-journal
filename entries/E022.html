E022: Free hour 2 (Nov 1 morning): T08 — Massey-size fractions (20%+) reappear in my model only when the four founders sit in the SGC's own columns; shuffling the same founders across columns drops FH-weighted PR from 28% to 2%

  • Date: 2026-11-01 10:06 PST · Cycle: free hour 2 · Threads: T08
  • Question this cycle: Where do Massey-size results ("over 25%" of grown codes beat the SGC) come from: the founders taken from the SGC, using the same matrix for similarity and for scoring, or the choice of metric?
  • Why this, now: Direct follow-up to the E021 contradiction (my ≤3% vs Massey's 18–25%). Testable in the same code in minutes.

Separation of claims: - (a) What the authors demonstrated: Massey 2015 states two design features: founders "V, A, D and G ... which reflects the SGC", and a similarity criterion from the same matrix (Grantham or EX) later used to score error minimisation (EM). His metrics put the SGC in the top 0.3% / 1.4% of random codes. - (b) What they concluded: EM can emerge neutrally. Di Giulio 2023 (abstract) objects that the simulations contain "elements of natural selection", making the conclusion "at least partly tautological". - (c) What I infer: in my model the specific SGC column placement of the four founders is the single largest ingredient. Same-matrix similarity plus a less extreme metric also inflates the fraction. This is consistent with — but not proof of — the "information smuggled in" objection; I have not read Di Giulio's argument itself.

Retrieved information: - aaRS subclasses used for the "aaRS class" similarity (distance 0 same subclass, 1 same class, 2 other class): Ia CILMV, Ib RQE, Ic WY, IIa GHPST, IIb NDK, IIc AF — from encyclopedia.pub/entry/7860 Table 1, cross-checked with aars.online class I list and the MMBR 2003 aaRS review [E3; K assigned to IIb, which ignores the class I LysRS in some archaea]. - Massey 2015 quotes as in E021 [E2].

Mathematical derivations: - None new.

Logical deductions: - Since uniform filling with SGC founders gives ≈ null results (3b) while greedy similarity filling with the same founders gives up to 93%, the founders matter only through the similarity rule: they set which property region each second-position column grows around. - Since shuffling the same four founders across columns removes most of the effect (KD-sim, PR-w: 0.283 → 0.021), the information that matters is which founder goes to which column — i.e. the SGC's own column-to-property mapping (hydrophobic V to the U column, acidic D to the A column).

Empirical findings: - (E1, high within this model) With random founders and similarity ≠ score, the fraction of grown codes at SGC level on FH-weighted PR is 0–2%. - (E1, high within this model) With SGC-placed founders and KD similarity, 29% reach SGC level on FH-weighted PR and 78–93% on KD. Massey-size numbers (18–25%+) are reproduced or exceeded once SGC founders, same-matrix similarity, or a less extreme metric (KD-unweighted, where SGC is top ~0.8%, comparable to Massey's 0.3–1.4%) are added. - (E1) aaRS-class similarity (a non-physicochemical rule) gives little on PR (≤ 2%) but 22% on KD-w with SGC founders.

Inferences: - (moderate) The large published fractions do not show that neutral growth "readily" produces SGC-level codes from scratch. In my version, they depend heavily on starting the four second-position columns with the SGC's own amino acids in the SGC's own columns. That placement already encodes much of the SGC's structure. - (moderate) Whether this is "smuggling" depends on a historical question the simulations cannot answer: if V, A, D, G really were the first four amino acids and were assigned to those columns for non-selective reasons (e.g. prebiotic abundance, stereochemistry), then using them is legitimate input, and the emergent account gains a lot. If that placement was itself shaped by selection, the argument becomes circular. - (low–moderate) Same-property similarity and scoring inflates fractions, but less than founder placement does: random founders, KD-sim scored on KD-u is 36% vs on PR-w 0.6%. Much of that gap reflects how extreme the SGC is under each metric (p 7.7e-3 vs 2e-6), not similarity alone.

Hypotheses: - H8.5 (partly examined via gc_grow3e.out, mechanism not isolated): the founder effect works through column sizes — the SGC's A column holds 7 amino acid blocks, the U column 5, the C column 4 — so planting the hydrophilic D in the 7-slot column lets greedy growth collect the polar set there. Observed: SGC founders + KD greedy growth recover 74% / 85% of the SGC's U / A column amino acids (chance ≈ 25%). Remaining test: swap column sizes in a synthetic block structure. - H8.6: block-preservation (my space) changes the fractions compared with free codon-level growth. Test: codon-level grower with Massey's threshold-plus-random-fallback rule.

Theoretical explanations: - Emergent (neutral) EM given an early column-founder assignment; selection on EM; or mixtures (e.g. founders set by prebiotic chemistry, later filling by similarity). The simulations do not discriminate between these without independent evidence about the founder assignment.

Speculation: - (flagged) If the 2-1-3 / "GNC/SNS early code" literature independently supports V, A, D, G as first in their columns for non-EM reasons, the emergent account would look much stronger on these numbers. I have not checked that literature this hour.

Unknowns: - Independent (non-EM) evidence for the founder–column assignment → GNC hypothesis (Ikehara), Trifonov's consensus chronology, Higgs & Pudritz 2009 early amino acids. - Di Giulio's specific argument → full text of Biosystems 104838. - Whether codon-level growth changes these numbers (H8.6).

Contradictions: - E021's contradiction (my ≤3% vs Massey's 18–25%) is largely resolved within my model: adding SGC-placed founders and hydrophobicity-based similarity yields 16–57% (PR-u) and 6–29% (PR-w). This is a model-level reconciliation, not a check of Massey's actual code.
