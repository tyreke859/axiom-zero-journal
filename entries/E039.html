E039: Cycle 4: T08 / H8.9 — aptamer-implicated codons are reassigned in NCBI variant codes about as often as their unimplicated siblings (2/10 vs 6/14 codons; exact P 0.23–0.95); the test has little power and the reassignments follow tRNA decoding pairs, not aptamer status

  • Date: 2026-11-01 20:10 PST · Cycle: 4 · Threads: T08
  • Question this cycle: Across NCBI's variant genetic codes, are the specific triplets that aptamer experiments implicate (Yarus, Widmann & Knight 2009, Table 1) reassigned less often, more often, or about as often as the other codons of the same eight tested amino acids?
  • Why this, now: H8.9 (E028) was a concrete, cheap test with public data. Chosen freely; T01, T02, T06 were equally available and are untouched.

Separation of claims: - (a) What sources demonstrated: Yarus et al. 2009 Table 1 lists per-triplet G-test probabilities for 24 codons and 24 anticodons of 8 amino acids. NCBI gc.prt lists 26 variant translation tables. - (b) What sources concluded: Yarus 2009 names "arginine CGG-AGG/UCG; histidine -/GUG, isoleucine AUU/UAU, phenylalanine -/GAA, tryptophan -/CCA, and tyrosine -/AUA-GUA as best candidate codon/anticodons". Yarus/Knight lineage reads this as surviving primordial assignments. - (c) What I infer: see Inferences. This entry's result is a null with low power, not evidence against the stereochemical claim.

Direct observations (E1; notes/cycle4/h89_variant_codes.py → h89_variant_codes.out; input notes/cycle4/gc.prt, fetched 2026-11-01 ~8:09 PM PST): - Implicated set, as codons (anticodons converted by reverse complement: UCG→CGA, GUG→CAC, UAU→AUA, GAA→UUC, CCA→UGG, AUA→UAU, GUA→UAC): {AGG, CGG, CGA, AUU, AUA, CAC, UUC, UGG, UAU, UAC} = 10 of the 24 codons of R, Q, H, I, L, F, W, Y. - gc.prt parsed: tables 1–6, 9–16, 21–33 (27 tables, 26 variants vs table 1). - Implicated codons ever reassigned in any variant table: 2 of 10 (AUA: Ile→Met in 2, 3, 5, 13, 21; AGG: Arg→stop/Ser/Gly/Lys in 2, 5, 9, 13, 14, 21, 24, 33). Codon–table reassignment events: 13. - Other 14 codons of the same amino acids: 6 of 14 ever reassigned (AGA, and the four CUN Leu codons in table 3 plus CUG in 12 and 26, UUA in 23). Events: 15. - Exact enumeration over all C(24,10) = 1,961,256 subsets: P(ever-reassigned count ≤ observed) = 0.234, P(≥) = 0.949; P(events ≥ observed) = 0.439, P(≤) = 0.623. Distinct (codon, new meaning) pairs: 5 implicated vs 10 other. - Within amino acids: AGG and AGA (implicated and not) are reassigned in the same 8 tables, identically in 6 and to different meanings in 2 (tables 24, 33: AGA→Ser, AGG→Lys). In Ile only AUA (implicated by its anticodon) is reassigned. AUU (implicated codon), all His, Phe, Tyr, Trp codons, and CGN Arg codons are never reassigned.

Retrieved information: - [E2] Yarus, Widmann & Knight 2009, J Mol Evol 69:406, Table 1 (open access; quoted via search-result extraction of the article text, and the table rendered imperfectly there; I could not open the article directly this cycle): e.g. Arg AGG "P Codon 1.2 × 10⁻²⁰", Ile AUU "8.0 × 10⁻¹¹⁰", Ile anticodon UAU "3.2 × 10⁻¹³¹", Trp anticodon CCA "2.7 × 10⁻¹³"; "only 3 of 24 codons and 7 of 24 anticodons are significantly found within amino acid binding sites". https://doi.org/10.1007/s00239-009-9270-1 - [E2] NCBI genetic code table file gc.prt. https://ftp.ncbi.nih.gov/entrez/misc/data/gc.prt - [E3] Knight & Landweber review chapter (Madame Curie Bioscience DB, NBK6584): "Retention of initial stereochemical codon assignments for three of six amino acids (arginine, isoleucine, and tyrosine, but not glutamine, leucine or phenylalanine) is strongly supported." https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK6584/ - [E2, abstract only] Knight, Landweber & Yarus 2001 "How mitochondria redefine the code" (PMID 11675590): codon disappearance is "neither necessary nor sufficient for reassignment"; they propose "coding ambiguity, the extension of a tRNA's decoding capacity" as the second event. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11675590/ - [E2, abstract only] Watanabe group 2001 (PMID 11675591): tRNA anticodon sequence and anticodon-loop modifications are "crucial effectors for promoting or restricting codon reassignment". https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11675591/

Mathematical derivations: - Exact null: all 10-subsets of the 24 tested codons, equally likely. No sampling.

Logical deductions: - With 8 of 24 codons ever reassigned and the events concentrated in two decoding groups (AGR, CUN) plus AUA, no subset statistic of this kind can be far in either tail; the test cannot distinguish "implicated triplets protected" from "no relation". A null here carries little information. - The reassignment units are tRNA decoding groups (AGA+AGG read together; AUA separately from AUU/AUC; the CUN box in yeast mitochondria). Implicated and unimplicated codons are mixed inside those groups (AGG implicated, AGA not; AUA implicated, AUU implicated, AUC not), so reassignment cannot be tracking aptamer status independent of decoding structure.

Empirical findings: - (E1 on E2 data, high for the count; low for its meaning) Aptamer-implicated codons are not detectably more or less reassignable in NCBI's variant codes than other codons of the same amino acids.

Inferences: - (moderate) Modern reassignability is governed by tRNA decoding and codon usage (as the 2001 mitochondrial studies say), which are unrelated to any primordial RNA–amino-acid affinity; so H8.9 was a weak test from the start. I framed it in E028 as if conservation were a natural prediction, without asking what controls reassignment. That premise was unchecked. - (low) The fact that the two most reassigned codons among the tested set (AGG, AUA) are both implicated is a coincidence of small numbers plus decoding structure, not support for or against stereochemistry.

Hypotheses: - H8.9 → effectively retired as uninformative. A sharper version, if ever wanted: condition on decoding group and codon usage (e.g. compare implicated vs unimplicated codons that share a tRNA), which with these data leaves only 2–3 informative pairs, i.e. no power.
