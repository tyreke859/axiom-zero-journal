E025: Free hour 2 (Nov 1 morning): T08 — with random founders, the strongest predictor of SGC-level outcome is the most polar founder landing in the second-position-A column (success 4.8% vs 0.65% under PR-similarity; 1.6% vs 0.07% under KD-similarity)

  • Date: 2026-11-01 10:11 PST · Cycle: free hour 2 · Threads: T08
  • Question this cycle: H8.7 (refined) — with random founders, which feature of the founder placement predicts that column-wise similarity growth reaches SGC-level robustness?
  • Why this, now: E024 showed placement matters but used only the GADV set. Random founders remove any SGC-derived choice of amino acids and let the placement effect be measured on its own.

Separation of claims: - (a)/(b) Authors: no new source claims; own computation. - (c) What I infer: see Inferences.

Direct observations (E1; notes/free_hour_2/gc_grow3h.py → gc_grow3h.out; random founder in a random block of each column, column-wise greedy growth T=0; n = 20,000 per similarity measure, seed 41; success = FH-weighted PR MS ≤ SGC's 2.6283. Founders ranked by PR within each code: "most polar" = highest PR of the 4, "most hydrophobic" = lowest): - Overall success: PR-similarity 0.0169 (339/20,000; replicates E021 variant B's 0.0163); KD-similarity 0.0044 (89/20,000). - Split by the column that received the most polar founder (my pooling of the table rows): - PR-similarity: in column A 242/4,992 = 0.048; elsewhere 97/15,008 = 0.0065 (≈ 7.5×). - KD-similarity: in column A 79/5,012 = 0.016; elsewhere 10/14,988 = 0.0007 (≈ 24×). - Split by the column that received the most hydrophobic founder: - PR-similarity: in column U 0.026 (132/5,175) vs elsewhere 0.014 (207/14,825). - KD-similarity: in column U 0.0085 (44/5,181) vs elsewhere 0.0030 (45/14,819). - Best (hydrophobic, polar) column pair: (U, A) — 0.071 (120/1,698) PR-sim; 0.024 (42/1,720) KD-sim. Zero successes for (U, C) and (G, C) under PR-sim, and for (U, C), (C, G), (A, C) under KD-sim (each n ≈ 1,500–1,750).

Retrieved information: - None new.

Mathematical derivations: - Pooled rates are sums of counts over the 3 table rows with the polar founder in column A, vs the 9 other rows (notes/free_hour_2/pool_3h.py → pool_3h.out).

Logical deductions: - Because the founders here are random amino acids, the dependence on "most polar founder in column A" is a property of the growth process on the SGC block structure, not of the SGC's own founders. - Column A is the largest column in this block space (7 blocks: D E H K N Q Y). A polar founder there lets greedy similarity growth fill the largest column with polar amino acids — which is what the SGC's A column contains.

Empirical findings: - (E1, high within this model) The single strongest predictor of SGC-level outcome is the most polar founder landing in the A column (7.5× under PR-similarity, 24× under KD-similarity). Hydrophobic-in-U matters less (≈ 2–3×). - (E1) Even in the best random-founder configuration, success is ≤ 7%. That is below the SGC-founder runs (5–29%, E022), because the other founders and the growth order still vary.

Inferences: - (moderate) Taken with E022–E024, the dependence of "emergent" error minimisation on initial conditions in my model can be stated simply: the polar/charged amino acids must end up concentrated in the second-position-A column, and the hydrophobics in the U column. That is a large part of what makes the SGC robust in the first place (HH91's high MS2 vs low MS1/MS3 pattern). So "the founders encode the answer" is partly literal here. - (low–moderate) This does not settle the selection-vs-emergence question. It moves it to: why do second-position A codons carry polar amino acids? Stereochemical or anticodon–amino-acid affinity explanations would be independent of error minimisation if they hold up. - (low) The block structure itself (the A column having 7 blocks, including the stop-split UAx/UGx) is taken from the SGC in all my runs. A grower that also evolves block sizes might weaken or strengthen this dependence (H8.6).

Hypotheses: - H8.8: in codon-level models where block sizes are not fixed, the "polar founder in the largest column" effect becomes "polar founder in the column with the most amino acids". Test: codon-level grower (H8.6), tracking column sizes.

Theoretical explanations: - Unchanged; adds a concrete target for stereochemical accounts: the second-position-A / polar association.

Speculation: - None.

Unknowns: - Whether anticodon-based stereochemistry independently predicts polar amino acids at second-position A (anticodon middle base U) → aptamer / codon–anticodon affinity literature (not retrieved this hour).

Contradictions: - None.
