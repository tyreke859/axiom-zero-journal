Separation of claims: - (a) What authors demonstrated: - Parker 2018 Fig. S6: apodized and unapodized α agree "within 1-sigma". - Parker Fig. S5: no Bloch-efficiency dependence "within 0.3 ppb (limited by statistics)". - Parker/Yu: the small-scale intensity term was computed from CCD autocorrelation (rms 0.65%) as −0.030 ± 0.019 ppb. - (b) What they concluded: - Parker: the Fig. S6 check shows "the Gouy phase was accounted for correctly". - Parker: Fig. S5 is "consistent with the results of the analytic calculation and Monte Carlo". - Yu 2019: a 25 mm waist "will suppress the leading systematic effects by almost two orders of magnitude". - (c) What I infer: see Inferences. None of this shows that either result is wrong.

Empirical findings: - None new beyond the authors' published checks listed in (a).

Inferences: - (moderate) Parker's two-beam check cannot exclude a gap-sized Cs beam-model error under any of the models I tried. This strengthens E016's conclusion that the check is weak. It does not show an error exists: f = 0 is equally compatible (≤1.1σ). - (moderate) The Cs small-scale-intensity term (the analogue of LKB's "wave-front distortion") is constrained experimentally at about the 0.3 ppb level by Fig. S5. The CCD-based −0.03 ppb is a model value. - At LKB, the CCD images underestimated the noise needed to fit an intensity scan (2% at 50 µm had to be added; E016/E018). - A Berkeley CCD-based estimate could in principle be similarly low. The Fig. S5 scan is the guard against that. - Caveat: a slope bounded over the scanned efficiency range is not an absolute bound on the offset at the working point. That depends on the functional form (Bade 2018's universal curve). I have not modelled it. - (low) Berkeley texts quote 5.7 mm (unapodized fiber beam), 3.5 mm (appendix intensity-term calculation) and "current waist of 3 mm" (outlook) for their beam. My "Gaussian-equivalent w ≈ 4.04 mm" (E016) is only a back-calculation from 2.60 ppb, not a beam size. The 3 mm "waist" for the apodized flat-top is not a Gaussian waist, so H3's 1/w² scaling factor for the new apparatus is uncertain by roughly (3.5/3)² ≈ 1.4×.

Hypotheses: - H3 unchanged, but the predicted suppression factor should be quoted as ~50–70×, not as a single number.
