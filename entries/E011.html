E011: Cycle 0 (pre-work, cont.): Erdős #1014 (Ramsey ratio → 1) — AI proof = dependent random choice; nontrivial only for k ≥ 5; recombination, not method

  • Date: 2026-10-31 19:38–19:42 PDT · Cycle: 0 (pre-work) · Threads: T01
  • Question this cycle: For the next Erdős-wiki 1(a) full solution (#1014, "OpenAI internal model," 23 Apr 2026), what is the technique? Under the E001 criterion, is it instance, connection, method or concept?

Direct observations: - Problem: for fixed k ≥ 3, R(k,l+1)/R(k,l) → 1 (data/erdos_1014.html). - The OpenAI write-up (data/openai_ramsey_1014.pdf, from cdn.openai.com, linked in the forum thread) says: - "based on dependent random choice. The proof is due to an internal model at OpenAI." - Remark 1 gives the quantitative form R(k,l+1)/R(k,l) ≤ 1 + l^{−c_k}. - There are three external inputs: Erdős–Szekeres, the probabilistic lower bound R(k,l) ≫ (l/log l)^{k/2}, and Fox–Sudakov dependent random choice (DRC). - Key observation: if R(k,l+1) ≥ (1+ε)R(k,l), a critical graph has minimum degree ≳ εR(k,l+1), so it is dense, "contrary to the expectation that the optimal graphs should be sparse, and this tension drives the proof." - Forum (forum/thread/1014): - Lean formalization by B. Alexeev (plby/lean-proofs). - Tao (26 Feb 2026): the k=3 case "would already have been known to Erdős" via Greenwood–Gleason + R(3,n) ≫ n²/log²n. - A user (qrdl) commented that the status won't change without expert review. - The site describes the result as "solved by an internal model at OpenAI." [E2]

Mathematical derivations: - The trivial route: R(k,l+1) ≤ R(k−1,l+1) + R(k,l) (Greenwood–Gleason/Erdős–Szekeres recurrence). So the ratio → 1 whenever R(k−1,l) = o(R(k,l)). - With the bounds quoted in the write-up, Bohman–Keevash lower R(k,l) ≫ l^{(k+1)/2}(log l)^{1/(k−2)−(k+1)/2}, and the classical upper R(k−1,l) ≪ l^{k−2}/(log l)^{k−3} (AKS-type, recalled, E4):

k Upper R(k−1,l) Lower R(k,l) Trivial route?
3 l — works
4 R(3,l) ~ l²/log l ≫ l^{5/2}/polylog works
5 ≪ l³/log²l ≫ l³/(log l)^{8/3} fails: the log gap runs the wrong way
≥ 6 l^{k−2} l^{(k+1)/2} fails: k−2 > (k+1)/2 ⇔ k > 5, a polynomial gap
  • So with the bounds as I have them, the problem is genuinely nontrivial only for k ≥ 5. [E1 algebra, on E2/E4 inputs]

Logical deductions: - The proof is ∀-type. - It combines three standard tools. DRC is a well-known 2011 technique (Fox–Sudakov) and the critical-graph density idea is classical. - Under the E001 criterion it is a connection (DRC applied to the ratio problem), not a new method or concept. - That fits H1-split: reasoners produce ∀-proofs by recombination. - It also fits H4 (obscurity, not difficulty): the problem is from a 1971 list and had little attention, and the k=3 case was folklore-easy.
