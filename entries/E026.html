E026: Free hour 2 (Nov 1 morning): T08 / H8.1 — SGC PR robustness is spread over 18 of 20 blocks; Asp and Tyr are the only worse-than-chance blocks (D–Y pair = 6.6% of weighted MS)

  • Date: 2026-11-01 10:12 PST · Cycle: free hour 2 · Threads: T08
  • Question this cycle: H8.1 — is the SGC's PR robustness spread across many blocks, or carried by a few? Which blocks are "badly placed"?
  • Why this, now: E021–E025 asked how a code could get robust. This asks where the robustness actually sits in the SGC. It is a pending open item (i), and it can be done in the remaining time with the existing E020 machinery.

Separation of claims: - (a)/(b) Authors: no new source claims; own computation. - (c) What I infer: see Inferences.

Direct observations (E1; notes/free_hour_2/gc_block.py → gc_block.out; same model and null as E020: 20 SGC blocks, stops excluded, random permutation of the 20 amino acids; 1,000,000 permutations, seed 20261101. Block contribution c_i = ½ Σ_j (C_ij + C_ji)(x_i − x_j)²; the c_i sum exactly to MS (checked: 5.1936 and 2.6283). Null mean of c_i from the first 200,000 permutations. "Leave-out" = block i's rows and columns set to zero in C, with the null recomputed the same way): - Whole code, this sample: unweighted p = 9.7e-5 (z = 2.79); FH-weighted p = 3.0e-6 (z = 3.71). E020 gave unweighted p = 1.19e-4 from a different 1M sample. 97 vs 119 hits is within about 2 Monte Carlo standard errors. - Block contribution relative to its null mean (SGC/null). FH-weighted: 18 of 20 blocks are below 1. The lowest are F 0.057, M 0.074, P 0.098, T 0.100, I 0.123, L 0.132, A 0.139. Only D (1.196) and Y (1.437) are above 1. Unweighted: F 0.068, P 0.182, T 0.251, S 0.257 lowest; above 1 are Y 1.222, E 1.363, D 2.028. - Largest single pair cost (FH-weighted): D–Y (GAY↔UAY, a first-position change; PR 13.0 vs 5.4) = 0.173, i.e. 6.6% of the SGC's total 2.6283. - Leave-one-block-out (FH-weighted): z stays in 2.98–3.62 for every block. The largest drops come from removing L (z 2.98, p 1.2e-4) or I (3.02); the code becomes more extreme when Y (3.62) or D (3.55) is removed (p 0 of 1M in both). Unweighted: z stays in 2.14–3.04. Removing S gives the lowest (2.14, p 1.2e-2); removing D gives the highest (3.04, p 1.6e-5).

Retrieved information: - None new.

Mathematical derivations: - MS = xᵀLx with L = diag(row sums + column sums) − (C + Cᵀ). The script asserts this equals the E020 ms() on the SGC and on 5 random codes.

Logical deductions: - No single block carries the result. Removing any one block leaves the weighted z between 3.0 and 3.6, compared with 3.71 for the full code. Because the null is recomputed each time, z is the comparable quantity, not p. - The hydrophobic second-position-U blocks (F, M, I, L) and the C-column blocks (P, T, A) contribute least relative to chance. These are the blocks where most single-base neighbours share the column property.

Empirical findings: - (E1, high within this model) The SGC's FH-weighted PR robustness is spread across nearly all blocks. 18 of 20 blocks contribute less than a randomly assigned amino acid would at the same position. The two exceptions are Asp and Tyr, whose shared first-position boundary (GAY/UAY) is the single most costly pair.

Inferences (E3, labelled): - E025 found that the most polar founder in the A column drives success in the growth models. The D/Y exception sits in that same A column: a hydrophobic-leaning aromatic (Y, PR 5.4) sits next to the most polar block (D, 13.0) one first-position change away. So the A column is "polar-filled" in the SGC, but not completely. Whether Tyr's placement reflects a late addition (as coevolution accounts would suggest) is not tested here. That would need a source check, which I did not do this hour.

Unknowns / not checked: - Results depend on PR and on FH98 weights, whose derivation I still could not access. I have not repeated the block analysis with KD. - Leave-out treats a removed block's codons like stops. That is one of several possible definitions.
