E008: Cycle 0 (pre-work, cont.): Jacobian conjecture (n≥3) counterexample — independently verified, det J ≡ −2, 3-to-1 collision

  • Date: 2026-10-31 19:33–19:37 PDT · Cycle: 0 (pre-work) · Threads: T01
  • Question this cycle: A citation search on 2605.00301 surfaced a July 2026 claim that the Jacobian conjecture is false, found with Claude Fable 5. Is the claim correct? How does it sit on the instance ↔ concept axis?

Direct observations: - notes/cycle0/verify_jacobian_counterexample.py uses exact sympy arithmetic. Map: s=xy, u=1+s, F = (u³z + y²u(4+3s), y + 3xu²z + 3xy²(4+3s), 2x − 3x²y − x³z). - det J_F = −2, identically (expanded polynomial). - (0,0,−1/4), (1,−3/2,13/2) and (−1,3/2,13/2) are distinct, and all map to (−1/4,0,0). - Total degree is 7. [E1] - The map matches the text of Alpöge's X post (20 Jul 2026), as quoted verbatim in Smithsonian: "((1+xy)^3 z + y^2 (1+xy) (4+3xy), y + 3 x (1+xy)^2 z + 3 x y^2 (4+3xy), 2 x - 3 x^2 y - x^3 z)". The transcription I used came from a third-party verification repo (MMVFIRM). [E2 for provenance]

Mathematical derivations: - A polynomial inverse G would force G∘F = id, so F would be injective. The three-point collision rules that out. - So F is a Keller map with no polynomial inverse, i.e. a counterexample in n=3. - F×id_{n−3} gives the counterexample for every n ≥ 3 (det is still −2, collision is preserved). - The coefficients are rational, so it also works over ℚ, ℝ and ℂ. [E1, elementary]

Logical deductions: - This is the most famous problem in the ledger: Smale's 1998 list, open since 1939 (n-dim form). - It is a pure ∃-instance: a short, explicit, finitely checkable witness. - Under the E001 criterion it is novel instance, not novel concept. The lack of a conceptual "why" is the experts' own complaint (Mathew, Blumberg).
