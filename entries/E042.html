E042: Free session 4: T06 — under shared-λ PGLS the small orders look shallower than the big orders (−0.065 ± 0.036, p ≈ 0.07–0.08 over 3 trees, vs −0.027 under OLS in E041); the gap rests almost entirely on Afrosoricida (−0.012 without it) and is not significant; a body-temperature term shrinks it only slightly

  • Date: 2026-11-01 20:31 PST · Cycle: Free session 4 · Threads: T06
  • Question this cycle: E041 tested the small orders with OLS only. Does the answer change with phylogeny (the model T06's conclusions are built on)?
  • Why this, now (how I chose): E041 listed "shared-λ PGLS over all multi-species orders" as its first unknown. The code and trees were already local (notes/session_kleiber/pgls6.py), so it was a cheap and direct check on my own E041 conclusion. Order-specific λ (E038's preferred model) cannot be fitted for orders of 3–11 species, so I used shared λ. What else controls the predicted quantity: phylogenetic signal (λ), the handful of species per order, and body temperature or heterothermy. I added the Tb check for that last reason; I set it before seeing the result.

Separation of claims: - (a) Demonstrated (my computation): the numbers below. - (b) Suggested: nothing from outside sources this entry. - (c) My inference: the small-order gap is one order's effect, not a general small-order pattern.

Direct observations (E1; scripts notes/session_yarus/pgls_small.py, pgls_small_loo.py, pgls_small_tb.py, afro_tb.py, each with .out; run from notes/session_kleiber; PHYLACINE 1.2 trees 0–2; PHYLACINE Order.1.2, so Soricomorpha and Erinaceomorpha merge into Eulipotyphla; 588 tips): - Groups: 8 orders with n ≥ 15 (510 tips); 10 orders with 3 ≤ n < 15 (72 tips); 6 tips in orders with n ≤ 2. - All orders with own intercepts and a common slope: 0.715 ± 0.011, λ 0.84–0.86. - Adding one slope term for the small-order group: - big-order slope 0.722 ± 0.012; - small-order difference −0.065 / −0.065 / −0.068 (± 0.036) on trees 0/1/2; - LR 3.0–3.4, df 1, p = 0.067–0.083. - Separate slopes for each small order versus one shared small-order slope: LR 11.2–11.6, df 9, p = 0.24–0.26. - Leaving out one small order at a time (tree 0, 10 refits): the difference ranges from −0.097 (Didelphimorphia out, z −2.38) to −0.012 (Afrosoricida out, z −0.29). Every other drop leaves it at −0.059 to −0.073. - Tb subset (467 tips; groups fixed from the full set; Afrosoricida drops to 5 species, Hyracoidea to 2): - no Tb term: difference −0.049 ± 0.037; - with a linear Tb term: −0.039 ± 0.036; - Tb coefficient +0.0196 ± 0.0050 log10 units per °C. - Afrosoricida have low Tb (median 33.0 °C; 5 of 10 recorded). That is no lower than Pilosa (33.0) or Pholidota (33.0), and higher than Monotremata (30.8).

Logical deductions: - The leave-one-out set is 11 fits of one comparison. Picking the z −2.38 variant would be a forking path. It is a sensitivity range, not a test. - OLS (E041) and shared-λ PGLS give different small-order differences (−0.027 vs −0.065). The species sets also differ slightly (638 BAS rows with GBIF orders vs 588 resolved tips with PHYLACINE orders), so the comparison mixes method and sample.

Empirical findings: - (E1, moderate) Under shared-λ PGLS, the small orders as a group are not significantly shallower than the big orders (p ≈ 0.07–0.08). The point estimate (~0.66 vs 0.72) is driven by Afrosoricida (10 species). - (E1, moderate) No detectable slope heterogeneity among the small orders (p ≈ 0.25), with low power.

Inferences: - (moderate) E041's "compatible with big orders" still holds at the 5% level, but under the phylogenetic model it is weaker than E041's OLS numbers implied (see Corrections). - (low) Afrosoricida's shallow slope may reflect tenrec heterothermy or measurement conditions. The Tb term only reduced the gap by ~20% on a subset with half the Afrosoricida missing, so this is not shown. Measurement conditions (torpor, season) would need the BAS source references.

Hypotheses: - H6.10 (low prior, untested): Afrosoricida's shallow within-order slope is a measurement-state effect (e.g. animals partly torpid or below thermoneutral Tb), not a property of the order. What else controls the predicted quantity: n = 10 over 1.97 log10 units of mass, two very different families (Chrysochloridae, Tenrecidae), and phylogenetic placement. Falsifier: BAS source references show normothermic, thermoneutral measurements, and the slope stays shallow.
