  • Date: 2026-10-31 19:36–19:39 PDT · Cycle: 0 (pre-work) · Threads: T01
  • Question this cycle: E005 found the minimal violators by brute force. Can they be explained in closed form? That tests how far the RL showcase is from hand mathematics (H5).

Direct observations: notes/cycle0/wagner_family_exact.py (sympy + numpy), output in wagner_family_exact_out.txt. - Family D(a,b): stars K_{1,a} and K_{1,b} whose centres are both joined to one middle vertex. n = a+b+3, and the matching number μ = 2. - The E005 violators are D(8,7) at n=18 and D(8,8) at n=19, matching the edge lists in wagner_trees_out.txt. - Computed λ1 values (exact, agreeing with numeric eigvalsh to 1e-6):

Mathematical derivations: - Eigen-equations (centres c1, c2, middle m): λc1 = a·c1/λ + m, λc2 = b·c2/λ + m, λm = c1 + c2. - Hence, with x = λ², 1 = 1/(x−a) + 1/(x−b). - In the symmetric case a=b, x = a+2. The violation condition √(a+2) + 2 < √(2a+2) + 1 becomes 2√(a+2) < a−1, i.e. (a−7)(a+1) > 0, i.e. a > 7. [E1, elementary] - So the family meets the bound with equality at n=17 and breaks it from n=18 (asymmetric (8,7)) onward. That explains why E005's exhaustive search found min = bound exactly at n=17.

Logical deductions: - The celebrated RL counterexample (Wagner 2021, Fig. 4) lies in a 2-parameter family whose λ1 follows from a quadratic, and the threshold a > 7 is one line of algebra. - A human who suspected "two stars plus a connector" would have found it by hand. - Context: Wagner says Stevanović's earlier counterexample had n = 600 vertices (Wagner p.5). Ghebleh et al. 2024, co-authored by Stevanović, still cite the RL 19-vertex example as the small one (ghebleh2024.pdf, l.73). So the hand family went unnoticed between 2010 and 2021, and the RL found a member of it. [E2]
