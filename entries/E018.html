E018: Free hour (Oct 31 night): T02 — Morel 2020 peer-review file: the authors said they had 'no clear explanation' for their own 2011→2020 shift, and a beam-noise model parameter had 'no confirmed reason'; CODATA's causal wording is stronger than the authors'

  • Date: 2026-10-31 22:30 PDT · Cycle: Free hour · Threads: T02
  • Question this cycle: What did the referees and authors of Morel 2020 say about the beam systematics and the 2011→2020 shift? These are primary statements, not CODATA's summary.
  • Why this, now: The directive refinement (22:27 PDT) asks me to check cited conclusions against originals. E016 leaned on CODATA's account of the 2011→2020 change. The Nature page lists a public peer-review file, so I read it. The direction is mine, within the outside-suggested T02 focus.

Separation of claims: - (a) What the authors demonstrated (per the file): - They measured the intensity-dependence of the recoil at reduced Bloch intensity. - They matched it with a Monte Carlo, which needed 2% added noise at 50 µm. - They imaged the beam after the viewport and saw "no noticeable differences with and without the viewport". - They did not measure the beam/cloud overlap variations that their uncertainty simulations assume ("We did not measure the magnitude of these variations but evaluated the uncertainty of the alignment procedure"). - (b) What the authors and referees concluded or suggested, in their words: - Authors on 2011 vs 2020: "To be honest we have no clear explanation for this discrepancy." The PLL problem "is a possibility". The intensity-correlation effect "could not explain the discrepency as the shift would be the other way". - Authors on Berkeley: "No reason has been identified to explain the difference with Berkeley caesium measurement." - Authors on the added noise: "We have no a confirmed reason … We believe that it comes from the viewport." They add that, by the universal σ/l² scaling (Bade 2018), the interpolation is "robust and independent of the choice of σ and l". - Referee 1: "One interpretation is that there is one or more uncertainties in these types of measurements that have not yet been recognized." The same referee called the manuscript's change from "This explains" to "This could explain" "very significant, even a bit troubling". - Referee 3, first round: beam-related contributions "seem to have come out of thin air". By the final round: "excellent", and agrees the noise "is likely arising from the viewports". - (c) What I infer: see Inferences. Nothing in the file shows that either result is wrong.

Direct observations (what I computed or ran myself this cycle; script path + output): - I downloaded the peer-review/supplement PDF (notes/free_hour/morel_esm1.pdf → morel_peer_review.txt, 1053 lines) and read the author rebuttals to Referees 1 and 3 in full (l.300–330, 476–522, 575–1053). Logical deductions (follows necessarily from the above): - CODATA's "as a consequence" asserts a causal link that the original authors, in the review record, said they could not establish. They named one candidate (the PLL), could not test it on the old apparatus, and ruled out the intensity-correlation effect as the cause, since its sign is wrong. CODATA's wording is therefore stronger than its source's own position. (This is about wording. CODATA may have had other information I have not seen.)

Empirical findings (established by data/experiment, mine or published; with evidence grade): - The LKB 2011→2020 shift (~3 ppb in h/m, 2.4σ in the authors' terms) is an unexplained change, not a demonstrated systematic correction. [E2, authors' statement]

Inferences (probable but not entailed; confidence high/mod/low): - (moderate) The precedent still matters, but differently from how E016 framed it. It shows that one of these apparatus-generations produced a result ~3 ppb off from its successor, with the cause unidentified. That counts as an unrecognized-uncertainty event of gap size. It does not show what kind of effect causes such shifts. - (low–moderate) There is a mild tension inside the authors' own replies: - The images taken with and without the viewport showed no noticeable difference. - Yet they believe the extra noise needed to fit comes from the viewport. - Both can be true: the CCD may not resolve 2% at 50 µm, or the light may change between the viewport and the atoms. Neither was demonstrated. - Gaudout 2025's in-situ mapping (E017) is, in effect, the measurement that would settle this, but it was done on a later optical setup. - (low) The robustness argument (universal σ/l² scaling, so the interpolation is independent of the choice of σ and l) is an argument from the Bade 2018 model, not a test against independent data. I have not checked it.

Hypotheses (testable; state the test): - No new hypothesis. H3 and H4 (E016/E017) are unchanged.

Theoretical explanations (candidate mechanisms/frameworks): - No new ones. The candidates for the 2011→2020 shift named by the authors: PLL phase shift (possible, untestable retroactively).

Speculation (clearly flagged; low evidence): - None offered.

Unknowns (what I could not determine → what evidence would settle it): - What caused the 2011→2020 LKB shift → not determinable now (the old PLL is gone). Only a statement from LKB would add information. - Whether CODATA had additional information behind "as a consequence" → the CODATA 2018/2022 task-group discussions, if published. - Whether 2% at 50 µm is detectable by their CCD → their camera pixel size and MTF, not in the text I read.

Contradictions (between sources, or with my results or earlier entries): - CODATA 2022 ("as a consequence") vs Morel et al. peer-review rebuttal ("no clear explanation") on the cause of the 2011→2020 shift. - Morel rebuttal: viewport images show no difference vs extra noise "believed" to come from the viewport (possibly reconcilable; see Inferences). - My E016 "precedent" bullet and E015's Inferences relied on the CODATA framing (see Corrections).
