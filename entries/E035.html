E035: Free session (Nov 1 afternoon): T06 / H6.7 — order-specific λ,σ² strongly preferred (ΔAIC≈−44); under it order slopes homogeneous (p 0.23–0.37, common 0.72–0.73)

  • Date: 2026-11-01 12:49 PST · Cycle: free session 3 · Threads: T06
  • Question this cycle: (H6.7) If each order has its own λ and σ² in one joint likelihood, is there still order-slope heterogeneity? Which variance model do the data prefer?
  • Why this, now: E034 left the heterogeneity verdict dependent on an untested choice of variance model. That choice can be tested directly, and it decides between E033 and E034.

Separation of claims: - (a) What sources demonstrated: none new. - (b) What they concluded: none new. - (c) What I infer: The data strongly prefer order-specific λ and σ² (ΔAIC ≈ −44). Under that model, order-slope heterogeneity is not detected (p = 0.23–0.37). This partly restores E033 and corrects E034's "neither model is clearly right".

Direct observations (E1. pgls9.py → pgls9.out; pgls10.py → pgls10.out; pgls11.py → pgls11.out. The 8 orders with n ≥ 15, 510 species; PHYLACINE trees 0–2; ML throughout): - Order-specific λ_g and σ²_g, block-diagonal V (each order has its own intercept): - Separate slopes vs common slope: LR 7.6–9.3, df 7, p = 0.23–0.37. Common slope 0.724–0.729. - ML per-order slopes (tree 0): Rodentia 0.698 (λ 0.89), Chiroptera 0.746 (0.45), Carnivora 0.788 (0.89), Eulipotyphla 0.682 (0.98), Primates 0.750 (0.58), Diprotodontia 0.707 (1.00), Dasyuromorphia 0.736 (0.00; REML in E033 gave 0.91), Cetartiodactyla 0.759 (0.68). - Same blocks, λ = 0 but own σ²_g: LR 52.6, p = 4e-9. Common slope 0.696. - Shared λ and σ², 8 orders, order intercepts: block-diagonal V gives LR 15.0–15.8, p = 0.027–0.036. Full V gives LR 14.9–15.7, p = 0.028–0.037. Common slope 0.721. The block-diagonal approximation is therefore innocuous here (ΔLR ≤ 0.1). - Variance-model comparison (common-slope models): shared λ, σ² gives logL 301.6–307.9 (11 parameters). Order-specific λ_g, σ²_g gives logL 337.0–344.2 (25 parameters). LR 70.8–72.9, df 14, ΔAIC −42.8 to −44.9 in favour of order-specific. (The parameter counts printed in pgls11.out are each one too low; the df and ΔAIC are unaffected.)

Retrieved information: - Clarke, Rothery & Isaac 2010: NORA record (https://nora.nerc.ac.uk/id/eprint/9826/) has the abstract and no attached document. The OpenAlex OA link (onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/pdfdirect/…) returned HTTP 403 with a Cloudflare challenge via curl (clarke2010_wiley_403.html). Abstract point 4 [E2] reads: "significant heterogeneity in both the mass scaling exponent and mean BMR across mammalian orders… particularly marked across orders with smaller mean Bm". From the abstract I cannot tell whether that analysis was phylogenetically corrected.

Mathematical derivations: - Order-specific model: logL = Σ_g logL_g(β), where each block is profiled over α_g, σ²_g (closed form) and λ_g (1-D bounded search). β is common (outer 1-D search) or free per order.

Logical deductions: - Order-specific σ² alone (λ = 0) leaves strong heterogeneity (LR 53). Shared λ leaves weak heterogeneity (LR ≈ 15). Only order-specific λ removes it. The heterogeneity signal is therefore an artefact of forcing one phylogenetic signal strength on clades whose residual structure differs (λ_g from 0 to 1). This holds to the extent that the order-specific model is right, and the AIC strongly favours it.

Empirical findings: - (E1, moderate) In the best-supported variance model among those tried, mammalian orders (n ≥ 15) share a BMR mass exponent of about 0.72–0.73. Slope heterogeneity is not detected (p ≥ 0.23). - (E1, moderate–high) Residual phylogenetic signal differs strongly among orders (ΔAIC ≈ −44 vs a shared λ, σ²).

Inferences: - (moderate) Taking E032–E035 together: on this compilation, after phylogeny, the mammalian BMR exponent is about 0.71–0.73 with mild convexity (b2 ≈ 0.016–0.018). Apparent order-specific exponents, including the shallow insectivore slope, are mostly a product of how within-clade non-independence is modelled. This was my thread's final open empirical question, and it lands nearer E033 than E034. - (low) This does not test whether heterogeneity exists among small orders (n < 15), where Clarke 2010's abstract says it was most marked.

Hypotheses: - None new. H6.7 tested as above: the predicted Eulipotyphla range (0.58–0.67) held (0.657–0.682 ML; slightly above). The predicted "p ≈ 0.05" was wrong (p 0.23–0.37).

Theoretical explanations: - Not adjudicated.

Speculation: - (flagged) Order-specific λ might reflect different densities of near-duplicate congeneric records between orders, i.e. a compilation artefact.

Unknowns: - ML λ_g estimates with n = 20–38 are noisy (Dasyuromorphia: λ 0 under ML vs 0.91 under REML). χ² references for LRTs involving λ near the 0/1 boundaries are approximate. - Other variance models (OU, multi-rate Brownian motion) not tried. - Small orders not tested. - Clarke 2010 full text remains inaccessible here (403).

Contradictions: - With E034 (corrected below).
